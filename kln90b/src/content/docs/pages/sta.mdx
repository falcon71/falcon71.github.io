The STA pages are the status pages of the KLN 90B. STA 1 shows the GPS state and the satellites, STA 2 the estimated position error, STA 3 the software revision status, STA 4 the total operational time and STA 5 a prediction of <Term t="RAIM" /> for a destination. You open them when the unit does not navigate, when it warns about its satellites, and when you want to know before an approach whether RAIM will be there.

## The STA pages: status of the receiver and the unit

The STA pages follow the CAL pages on the left side. [Controls and display](/kln90b/unit/controls-and-display/#selecting-a-page) explains how you select pages.

STA 1 to STA 4 only show values. They have no field for the cursor, so the left <kbd>CRSR</kbd> does nothing on them. STA 5 is the one STA page that you operate: it has three fields, and [the cursor and data entry](/kln90b/unit/controls-and-display/#entering-data-with-the-cursor) work there as everywhere in the unit.

STA 1 and STA 2 follow the receiver and change as it does. STA 3 and STA 4 show their values as they were when you selected the page.

How the receiver finds its position, what its states mean and what RAIM is belongs to [GPS reception and RAIM](/kln90b/operation/gps-receiver/). The sections below explain the pages and their fields.

## STA 1: GPS state and satellites

STA 1 shows the GPS state and the satellites the receiver hears, with their signal strength and elevation. It is the first page to open when the unit does not navigate: the state in the top line says whether the unit has a position, and the list says which satellites it receives and how well.

<KlnScreen screen="sta-1-nav" />

The list holds up to eight satellites in the order of their numbers, four on a page. [The GPS states](/kln90b/operation/gps-receiver/#the-gps-states) says what each state means.

### The second page of the list

The list has a second page as soon as it holds a fifth satellite, and the status line then shows `STA+1` (several pages with one name, see [Controls and display](/kln90b/unit/controls-and-display/#selecting-a-page)). The second page repeats the column headings and shows the fifth to the eighth satellite.

<KlnScreen screen="sta-1-nav-2" />

When the list falls back to four satellites or fewer while the second page is shown, the unit returns to the first page, and the name loses its `+`.

### While the unit has no position

When the unit has no position, the state reads `ACQ`, `INIT` or `TRAN`. During the acquisition after power-on, the list shows the satellites that the unit expects to find, from the position and the time it remembers, with the elevation it predicts for them. Every satellite starts with a `*` and a signal strength of `00`; each loses the `*` and gains a signal strength when the receiver takes it into the position.

<KlnScreen screen="sta-1-acq" />

During `INIT` the list shows the satellites that the receiver searches without knowing where they stand, so their elevations are dashes until the unit has the ephemeris data of the satellite. A unit that loses its position in flight goes back to `ACQ` at once, and its list then shows the satellites it still tracks with their real elevations; [Losing the position in flight](/kln90b/operation/gps-receiver/#losing-the-position-in-flight) describes what the unit does then.

### A failed receiver

With a failed receiver or antenna the state reads `FAILR` and the list is empty, so the page has no second page. STA 2 shows dashes.

<KlnScreen screen="sta-1-failed" />

[Simulated failures](/kln90b/operation/failures/#the-receiver-and-the-antenna) describes the failures of the receiver and the antenna. [GPS reception and RAIM](/kln90b/operation/gps-receiver/) uses STA 1 to follow the receiver from power-on to a position.

## STA 2: estimated position error

STA 2 shows the estimated position error, the unit's own estimate in nautical miles of how far its position can be off. Look at it when the unit raises `BAD SATELLITE GEOMETRY` `SEE EPE ON STA 2 PAGE`, which happens en route or in the approach arm mode; in the approach active mode the unit raises `BAD SATELLITE GEOMETRY` `AND RAIM NOT AVAILABLE`, which does not name STA 2.

<KlnScreen screen="sta-2-epe" />

[The estimated position error](/kln90b/operation/gps-receiver/#the-estimated-position-error) explains what the <Term t="EPE" /> is made of. Without a position the page shows dashes instead of the number:

<KlnScreen screen="sta-2-no-position" />

## STA 3: software revision status

STA 3 shows the software revision of the unit, the software revision of the receiver and the OBS calibration. You need it only to check which release of the KLN 90B your aircraft runs, for example after an update.

<KlnScreen screen="sta-3" />

The values are fixed. Nothing on the page can be changed, and the revision of the unit is that of the release you installed.

## STA 4: total operational time and power-ons

STA 4 shows how many hours the unit has been on in all and how many times you have turned it on.

<KlnScreen screen="sta-4" />

The hours are whole hours, rounded down, and the value does not count up while the page is shown. Both values stay when you turn the unit off.

## STA 5: RAIM prediction

STA 5 predicts whether RAIM will be available at a destination at a time you choose, from 15 minutes before to 15 minutes after. You use it before an approach, and the unit sends you here when it cannot activate an approach for lack of RAIM.

<KlnScreen screen="sta-5-result" />

The prediction is for the destination and the time at the top of the page, which the page fills in for you when it can. The destination is the last waypoint of the active flight plan, counted from the active leg; when FPL 0 holds an approach, that is the missed approach point. With a direct-to or in OBS mode it is the active waypoint, and the field is blank when nothing is active. The ETA is the present time plus the time en route to the destination, which exists only while the groundspeed is above 2 kt: on the ground the ETA is blank, and you enter it yourself. The page fills in the destination and the ETA once, when you select it. A time that has already passed today counts as tomorrow.

{/* kln90b#473: describes the current behavior; the fix makes the unit keep the default ETA up to date while the page is shown */}

You can change the destination, the ETA and the time zone with the left cursor. The page does not keep your entries: when you leave it and select it again, it starts anew with the defaults.

The bar graph shows seven predictions, one for the ETA and one for each of six times around it at intervals of 5 minutes; the scale under the bars gives the minutes before and after the ETA. A bar above the line means that RAIM is predicted to be available at the destination, a bar below the line that it is predicted not to be. The prediction works without a position, so you can make it on the ground before you start.

### Calculating

Selecting the page and every new destination or ETA start a calculation, which takes 10 seconds. Until it is done, the line of the bar graph reads `COMPUTING`:

<KlnScreen screen="sta-5-computing" />

A new time zone changes only how the ETA is shown and starts no calculation.

### Without a destination or an ETA

While the page has no destination or no ETA, it calculates nothing, and the line of the bar graph holds a row of short marks. Enter the missing value:

<KlnScreen screen="sta-5-entry" />

### The ETA in another time zone

Enter the ETA with the time zone set to `UTC`. With another time zone the unit uses the hours and minutes you entered as if they were `UTC`, so the prediction is for the wrong time, while the ETA on the page shows what you entered.

{/* kln90b#472: describes the current behavior; the fix makes the unit predict for the ETA in the time zone shown */}

### What the result means for an approach

The prediction uses the limit of an approach, whatever the unit is doing now. When the unit refuses to activate an approach because of RAIM, it raises `RAIM NOT AVAILABLE` `APR MODE INHIBITED`, which sends you to STA 5. [Predicting RAIM](/kln90b/operation/gps-receiver/#predicting-raim) describes the task, and [Non-precision approaches](/kln90b/operation/approaches/) what RAIM decides in an approach.
