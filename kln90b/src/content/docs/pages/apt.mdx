The APT pages of the KLN 90B show everything the database holds about one airport, one kind of information per page. APT 1 to APT 6 give the airport's data and your remarks, APT 7 its SID and STAR procedures, and APT 8 its approaches. All of them show the same airport, which you choose on any of them.

## The APT pages: one airport on every page

Entering an identifier, scanning through the database and approving the waypoint page work as on every waypoint page; see [Waypoints and the database](/kln90b/operation/waypoints/).

The airport you enter, scan to or create on one APT page is the airport on all the others, and the unit remembers it when you turn it off. One exception is the airport of the last active waypoint: if that was an airport, the unit comes on with it, shown on APT 4. Another is an airport that you only approve or that is the active waypoint: the Active Waypoint pages and the waypoint page shown for approval show their own airport and leave the shared one alone.

The right inner knob goes through APT 1 to APT 7 and then back to APT 1; APT 8 follows APT 7 only in an aircraft whose unit is installed for approaches. A page number with more than one page shows a `+` in its name (`APT+3`); [Pages with a plus sign](/kln90b/unit/controls-and-display/#pages-with-a-plus-sign) explains how the inner knob steps through them.

The top line is the same on every page except the APT 3 runway diagram, which has no top line. It shows the identifier of the airport and, at the right end, `NR` (for nearest) and the rank, counted from `NR 1` for the nearest airport, when you reached the airport through the [nearest list](/kln90b/operation/nearest/). An airport of the list keeps its `NR` on APT 2 to APT 7; APT 8 shows its own text there. A `›` in the first column marks the airport as the [active waypoint](/kln90b/operation/modes-of-operation/#the-active-waypoint); it flashes during [waypoint alerting](/kln90b/operation/sequencing/#waypoint-alerting).

<KlnScreen screen="pages/apt/apt-1-nearest" rows="1" />

### User-defined airports

When the identifier you enter on an APT page matches no airport, the unit offers to create a user-defined airport; [Creating a user waypoint](/kln90b/operation/user-waypoints/#creating-a-user-waypoint) explains the offer and how you answer it. Whichever page made the offer, the new airport appears on APT 1. A user-defined airport has a position, an elevation and one runway, which you enter on APT 1 (the position), APT 2 (the elevation) and APT 3 (the runway); it has no name, no city and no frequencies. Like every airport, it can hold remarks on APT 5.

### The ACT pages of an airport

When the active waypoint is an airport, its APT pages appear as ACT 1 to ACT 7 and, where APT 8 exists, ACT 8.

## APT 1: name, position and airspace

APT 1 shows the name of the airport, its position and the airspace it lies in. It is the page where you check that you have the right airport, and where a new user-defined airport ends up.

<KlnScreen screen="pages/apt/apt-1-ksea" />

With the cursor on, you can change the identifier; on a nearest airport the cursor can also rest on the rank (see below). The name, the airspace row and the position of an airport of the database cannot be changed.

<KlnScreen screen="pages/apt/apt-1-ksea-cursor" />

### A nearest airport

The page changes when you reach the airport through the [nearest list](/kln90b/operation/nearest/#showing-a-nearest-list). The rank `NR 2` appears at the right of the top line, and the three rows below the name give the longest runway (length and surface), its lighting, and the bearing and distance from the aircraft in place of the position and the airspace. An airport that you enter by its identifier shows the plain page, even when it is the nearest.

<KlnScreen screen="pages/apt/apt-1-nearest" />

The page keeps the airport it shows, and its rank changes as the aircraft moves. When the airport drops out of the list, the rank disappears and the page shows the position and the airspace again. With the cursor on the rank, the page keeps the rank and shows whichever airport holds it.

<KlnScreen screen="pages/apt/apt-1-nearest-cursor" />

[Nearest](/kln90b/operation/nearest/) explains which airports the list holds, how it is renewed and how the cursor on the rank works.

### A user-defined airport

A user-defined airport shows only its identifier and its position. You can change the position with the cursor; the new value is stored when you press <kbd>ENT</kbd>.

<KlnScreen screen="pages/apt/apt-1-user-airport" />

With `USER POS?`, APT 1 opens with the cursor on the empty latitude, and the airport exists when you have entered both coordinates; [Creating a user waypoint](/kln90b/operation/user-waypoints/#creating-a-user-waypoint) shows the steps.

[Waypoints and the database](/kln90b/operation/waypoints/) and [Nearest](/kln90b/operation/nearest/) describe the tasks that use APT 1.

## APT 2: city, elevation, time zone and approaches

APT 2 shows where the airport is, how high it lies, which time zone it is in, and the best kind of approach it has. Use it to find out the local time difference and whether you can expect an instrument approach.

<KlnScreen screen="pages/apt/apt-2-ksea" />

Nothing on this page can be changed for an airport of the database. The row for the time zone stays blank where no time zone is known. The radar symbol `(R)` at the end of the approach row appears only for airports whose data marks radar coverage.

A user-defined airport shows only its elevation, as an entry, because the city, the time zone and the approaches are unknown.

<KlnScreen screen="pages/apt/apt-2-user-airport" />

[Waypoints and the database](/kln90b/operation/waypoints/) describes the tasks that use APT 2.

## APT 3: runways

APT 3 shows the runways of the airport, first as a diagram and then as a list of lengths, surfaces and lighting. Use the diagram to see how the runways lie to each other and the list for their data.

<KlnScreen screen="pages/apt/apt-3-diagram-kpdx" />

The diagram is the first APT 3 page of an airport that has runways, and the status line therefore reads `APT+3`. It shows no identifier and no top line, so the page has no field: the right <kbd>CRSR</kbd> does nothing, and you cannot change the identifier there. Scanning works. At an airport whose runway data lists a helipad, the diagram can stay blank, while the list pages work.

{/* kln90b#476: describes the current behavior (whether the simulator's own data has such runway entries is unverified); the fix makes the unit draw the diagram without them */}

The runway list follows: the unit lists at most the five longest runways, longest first, two runways per page.

<KlnScreen screen="pages/apt/apt-3-list-ksea" />

An airport without a runway entry has no diagram, and its one page reads `RUNWAY DATA NOT AVAILABLE`. If you enter an identifier that no airport has while a list page is shown, and then correct it to an airport that has a diagram, the unit shows that diagram and turns the cursor off, so that the next click of the right inner knob selects another page instead of changing a character.

{/* kln90b#475: describes the current behavior; the fix makes the unit stay on the list page with the cursor on */}

A user-defined airport has one page, which holds its single runway: the length and the surface, which you enter. The runway you enter decides whether the airport can be in the nearest list ([Setting the nearest airport criteria](/kln90b/operation/nearest/#setting-the-nearest-airport-criteria)).

<KlnScreen screen="pages/apt/apt-3-user-airport" />

[Waypoints and the database](/kln90b/operation/waypoints/) and [Nearest](/kln90b/operation/nearest/) describe the tasks that use APT 3.

## APT 4: communication frequencies

APT 4 lists the communication frequencies of the airport. The unit only shows them; you tune the radio yourself.

<KlnScreen screen="pages/apt/apt-4-kykm" />

Each line holds a four-character frequency type and a frequency in MHz with two decimals; the unit rounds the half up (128.075 shows as `128.08`). A frequency up to and including 30 MHz is a high frequency and shows in kHz without a decimal point. The page lists the frequencies in the order of the database, without sorting or merging them, so a frequency that the database lists twice shows twice. These are the labels:

| Frequency type | Frequency of |
| --- | --- |
| `ATIS` | Automatic terminal information service |
| `MCOM` | Multicom |
| `UNIC` | Unicom |
| `CTAF` | Common traffic advisory frequency |
| `GRND` | Ground control |
| `TWR` | Tower |
| `CLR` | Clearance delivery, also remote clearance delivery |
| `APR` | Approach control |
| `DEP` | Departure control |
| `CTR` | A center, where it provides approach and departure control |
| `AFIS` | A flight service station of the airport (aerodrome flight information service) |
| `AWOS` | Automated weather observing system |
| `ASOS` | Automated surface observing system |
| `PTAX` | Pre-taxi clearance |

A frequency of another type is listed without a frequency type. A frequency of no type is not listed.

The page shows five frequencies. An airport with more than five has more pages, and the status line reads `APT+4`; the inner knob steps through them. If you change the airport while a later page is shown and the new airport has fewer pages, the page shows the new identifier with blank lines until you turn the right inner knob one click counterclockwise.

{/* kln90b#474: describes the current behavior; the fix makes the unit show the first frequency page of the new airport */}

An airport without a usable frequency, and every user-defined airport, shows `COMM FREQ DATA NOT AVAILABLE`.

<KlnScreen screen="pages/apt/apt-4-no-data" />

[Waypoints and the database](/kln90b/operation/waypoints/) describes the tasks that use APT 4.

## APT 5: remarks

APT 5 holds three lines of notes of your own about the airport, for example a frequency or a procedure you want to remember, which the page shows whenever you choose that airport. The unit keeps them when it is turned off.

<KlnScreen screen="pages/apt/apt-5-ktiw" />

The unit keeps the remarks by identifier: if you delete a user-defined airport, its remarks stay, and a user-defined airport that you create again with the same identifier shows them again. It keeps the remarks of up to 100 airports. A remark for the 101st raises the message `RMKS FULL` (the remarks are full) and is not stored; changing the remarks of an airport already in the list is always accepted.

OTH 4 (`APTS W/RMKS`, airports with remarks) lists the airports that have remarks; you delete an airport's remarks there. An airport whose lines you have blanked out still counts as having remarks, stays on OTH 4 and counts toward the 100 until you delete it there.

[Waypoints and the database](/kln90b/operation/waypoints/) describes the tasks that use APT 5.

## APT 6: services

APT 6 is meant for the fuel, the oxygen, the customs and the fee information of the airport. The simulator holds none of it, so the page shows the same texts for every airport, user-defined airports included, and the line for customs stays blank.

<KlnScreen screen="pages/apt/apt-6-ksea" />

## APT 7: SID and STAR procedures

APT 7 lists the <Term t="SID" />s and <Term t="STAR" />s of the airport on the APT pages and
loads the one you select into FPL 0. You use it on the ground to load the SID, and en route to load the STAR
before the descent. APT 7 is part of every installation of the unit; only APT 8 depends on how the unit is installed.

An airport with SIDs and STARs has two APT 7 pages, and the status line shows `APT+7`: the first page lists
the SIDs, the second the STARs. Turning the right inner knob clockwise from APT 6 brings you to the SIDs first;
turning it counterclockwise from APT 8, or from APT 1 in a unit without APT 8, brings you to the STARs first. An
airport with only one kind of procedure has one APT 7 page with that list.

<KlnScreen screen="pages/apt/apt-7-stars-ksea" />

Press the right <kbd>CRSR</kbd> to turn the cursor on. It comes on at the first entry, or at the top entry shown when
you had scrolled the list before. The bottom row of the list always shows the last entry, and the rows above it scroll as you turn the right outer knob. The cursor flashes on an entry that <kbd>ENT</kbd> selects, and on
`LOAD IN FPL` and `APPROVE?` later on. With an expired database, each press of the right <kbd>CRSR</kbd> that turns the
cursor on shows `OUTDATED DB` on the status line; you can still select and load procedures.

<KlnScreen screen="pages/apt/apt-7-sids-ksea" />

An airport for which the database has no procedure the unit can fly shows this text instead of the lists:

<KlnScreen screen="pages/apt/apt-7-none" />

On ACT 7, when the active waypoint is an airport, the same lists of that airport appear, and you select and load a
procedure the same way. The airport identifier cannot be changed there.

### Selecting a SID or a STAR

You select a SID in the order you fly it: the SID itself, then the runway you take off from, then the
<Term t="Transition">transition</Term> to the en route fix. A STAR goes the other way: the STAR itself, then the
transition from the en route fix, then the runway you land on. The unit asks for a runway or a transition only when
there is a choice to make.

To load a SID or a STAR into FPL 0, the airport must be on the APT pages (see
[Waypoints and the database](/kln90b/operation/waypoints/)). Then:

1. Turn the right outer knob to the APT pages and the right inner knob to the SIDs or the STARs of APT 7. From APT 1,
   the STARs are two steps counterclockwise in a unit with APT 8, one step in a unit without it.
2. Press the right <kbd>CRSR</kbd> and turn the right outer knob until the cursor is on the procedure.
3. Press <kbd>ENT</kbd>. The unit shows the first list it needs, `RUNWAY` or `TRANSITION`, with the cursor on its
   first entry, or, when there is nothing to choose, the waypoints of the procedure (step 5).

   <KlnScreen screen="pages/apt/apt-7-runway" />

4. Turn the right outer knob to the runway or the transition and press <kbd>ENT</kbd>. If a second list follows,
   select its entry the same way.

   <KlnScreen screen="pages/apt/apt-7-transition" />

5. Check the waypoints of the procedure as they go into FPL 0, which the unit now shows with the cursor on
   `LOAD IN FPL`, and press <kbd>ENT</kbd>.

   <KlnScreen screen="pages/apt/apt-7-load" />

6. If the unit asks a question (below), press <kbd>ENT</kbd> to approve it. The unit loads the procedure and shows
   FPL 0 on the left side, so that you can check where it went. APT 7 shows its list again with the cursor off.

If you selected the wrong entry, press <kbd>CLR</kbd>: each press goes back one step, skips the steps the unit skipped
on the way forward, and puts the cursor on the entry you selected there before. While you select, the right cursor stays on by itself. If you turn it off and
turn the right inner knob to another page, the selection is dropped, and APT 7 shows its list when you come back.

**A transition must be selected whenever the procedure has one.** When a procedure has two or more transitions, the
list has no entry for the procedure without a transition, so you cannot load only the part that all transitions
share. A procedure with one transition gets it without a question.

<Callout kind="info">
A transition or an approach can put waypoints into FPL 0 twice. Only copies among the en route waypoints raise
[`REDUNDANT WPTS IN FPL`](/kln90b/operation/departures-arrivals/#redundant-wpts-in-fpl); copies between a STAR and an approach raise no message.
Look for them in FPL 0 yourself and delete them by hand.
</Callout>

The unit skips a step when it has nothing to ask:

- **The transition step** is skipped when the procedure has one transition, which the unit takes, or none.
- **The runway step** is skipped when every runway of the procedure leads to the same waypoints, because then the
  runway changes nothing in FPL 0. It is also skipped when the procedure has no part of its own for each runway.
  KYKM's YKM7 SID has neither a runway nor a transition to choose, so <kbd>ENT</kbd> on it shows its waypoints at
  once.

Each entry of the runway list is `RW` and the runway number, such as `RW10L`. Parallel runways that lead to the same waypoints share
one entry with `B` for the parallels, such as `RW10B`, and selecting it loads the first of them. Runways of different
numbers always have their own entries.

### Questions before the load

When you press <kbd>ENT</kbd> on `LOAD IN FPL`, the unit loads at once if FPL 0 already contains the airport and holds
no procedure of the same kind. If the airport is not in FPL 0, also when FPL 0 is empty, it asks whether to add the
airport with the procedure:

<KlnScreen screen="pages/apt/apt-7-add-airport" />

If FPL 0 already holds a procedure of the same kind, a SID when you load a SID or a STAR when you load
a STAR, the unit asks whether to replace it, also when it is the same procedure. This is then the only question:
if the airport is missing, the unit adds it without asking. A procedure of the other kind does not count: loading a
STAR while FPL 0 holds a SID asks nothing about the SID.

<KlnScreen screen="pages/apt/apt-7-replace" />

Press <kbd>ENT</kbd> to approve the question, or <kbd>CLR</kbd> to go back to the waypoints with FPL 0 unchanged.

### Where the procedure goes in FPL 0

The unit puts a SID right after its airport in FPL 0 and a STAR right in front of it; an airport it adds goes to the
start of FPL 0 for a SID and to the end for a STAR. [Where the procedure goes in FPL 0](/kln90b/operation/departures-arrivals/#where-the-procedure-goes-in-fpl-0)
gives the rules, and [SID and STAR procedures](/kln90b/operation/departures-arrivals/) how you change, delete and edit
the procedure there.

### What the unit keeps of a procedure

Of a procedure it loads, the unit keeps the legs that end at a waypoint;
[What the unit keeps of a procedure](/kln90b/operation/departures-arrivals/#what-the-unit-keeps-of-a-procedure) says
what you fly yourself. A DME arc becomes a curved leg that starts at a DME arc waypoint the unit computes
([DME arcs](/kln90b/operation/dme-arcs/)). The lists offer only the procedures the unit can fly: none with an
<Term t="RF leg" />, none that needs <Term t="RNP AR" />, and none in which no leg ends at a waypoint.

## APT 8: approaches

<Callout kind="availability">
APT 8 is available only if the unit is installed for non-precision approaches. Not every aircraft has such an
installation. Without it, the APT pages end at APT 7, and the unit has no ACT 8 page either.
</Callout>

APT 8 lists the non-precision approaches of the airport on the APT pages and loads the one you select, with its missed
approach, into FPL 0. You use it en route before the descent, usually after loading the STAR on APT 7.

<KlnScreen screen="pages/apt/apt-8-approaches-ksea" />

The bottom row of the list always shows the last approach. Press the right <kbd>CRSR</kbd> to turn the
cursor on: it comes on at the first approach, or at the top approach shown on a scrolled list. As on APT 7, an expired
database shows `OUTDATED DB` on the status line each time you turn the cursor on.

Each approach has a short name of eight characters in the list and a header that heads its waypoints on APT 8 and in
FPL 0:

| Approach | In the list | Header |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <Term t="RNAV" /> (GPS) to runway 34L | `RNAV 34L` | `R34L-KSEA` |
| GPS to a runway | `GPS` and the runway | `G`, the runway and the airport |
| VOR to runway 21 | `VOR  21` | `V21-KGEG` |
| VOR/DME to runway 27 | `VORD 27` | `D27-KYKM` |
| NDB or NDB/DME to runway 32R | `NDB  32R` | `N32R-KMWH` |
| VOR-A or VOR/DME-A (no runway) | `VOR-A` | `VOR-A-KYKM` |
| NDB-B (no runway) | `NDB-B` | `NDB-B-KHIO` |

An RNAV approach without a runway shows `RNV` and its letter, such as `RNVA`. The letter of an approach to a runway is
never shown, so RNAV (GPS) Y RWY 19 and RNAV (GPS) Z RWY 19 at KRLD both read `RNAV 19` and `R19-KRLD`. Tell them
apart by their IAFs and waypoints before you load one.

The unit offers RNAV (GPS) approaches that have <Term t="LNAV" /> minima, and GPS, VOR, VOR/DME, NDB and NDB/DME
approaches. It leaves out ILS, localizer, LDA and SDF approaches, RNAV approaches that have only LPV or LNAV/VNAV
minima, approaches that need RNP AR, and approaches with an RF leg anywhere, the missed approach included. Of KSEA's
approaches, the list shows the six RNAV approaches. An airport without an approach the unit can fly shows this:

<KlnScreen screen="pages/apt/apt-8-none" />

On ACT 8, when the active waypoint is an airport, the same list of that airport appears, and you select and load an approach the same way.

### Selecting an approach

You select an approach, then its <Term t="IAF" />, then you load it.

To load an approach into FPL 0, the airport must be on the APT pages (see
[Waypoints and the database](/kln90b/operation/waypoints/)). Then:

1. Turn the right outer knob to the APT pages and the right inner knob to APT 8, one step counterclockwise from APT 1.
2. Press the right <kbd>CRSR</kbd> and turn the right outer knob until the cursor is on the approach.
3. Press <kbd>ENT</kbd>. The unit shows the list after `IAF` with the cursor on the first entry, or, when the approach
   has one transition or none, its waypoints (step 5).

   <KlnScreen screen="pages/apt/apt-8-iaf" />

4. Turn the right outer knob to the IAF and press <kbd>ENT</kbd>.
5. Check the waypoints of the approach, which the unit now shows from that IAF to the end of the missed approach with
   the cursor on `LOAD IN FPL`: turn the right outer knob counterclockwise to scroll the list, and clockwise back down
   to `LOAD IN FPL`. The list stays scrolled.

   <KlnScreen screen="pages/apt/apt-8-load" />

   <KlnScreen screen="pages/apt/apt-8-load-scrolled" />

6. Press <kbd>ENT</kbd>.
7. If the unit asks a question (below), press <kbd>ENT</kbd> to approve it. The unit loads the approach and shows
   FPL 0 on the left side. APT 8 shows its list again with the cursor off.

<kbd>CLR</kbd> goes back one step at a time, as on APT 7, and puts the cursor on the IAF or the approach you selected
before.

**An approach starts only at an IAF:** no entry after `IAF` begins at the intermediate fix or later
([An approach starts only at an IAF](/kln90b/operation/approaches/#an-approach-starts-only-at-an-iaf),
[Vectors to final](/kln90b/operation/approaches/#vectors-to-final)). **A transition must be selected whenever the
approach has one,** because the entries after `IAF` are its transitions; an approach with one transition or none skips
the step. Each entry is named after the first waypoint of its transition. On a long list after `IAF`, `IAF` stays on
the top row and the bottom row always shows the last entry.

The waypoint list marks the fixes that have a role in the approach with a suffix after the identifier, such as `-I`
for the IAF ([The approach in FPL 0](/kln90b/operation/approaches/#the-approach-in-fpl-0) lists the suffixes). What the unit leaves out of an approach (legs without a waypoint, holding patterns
and procedure turns, altitude restrictions) is the same as for a SID or a STAR
([What the unit keeps of a procedure](/kln90b/operation/departures-arrivals/#what-the-unit-keeps-of-a-procedure)), and
so is its treatment of DME arcs ([DME arcs](/kln90b/operation/dme-arcs/)).

### Questions before loading an approach

The unit asks on APT 8 before it adds the airport or replaces an approach in FPL 0
([Loading an approach](/kln90b/operation/approaches/#loading-an-approach),
[Replacing an approach](/kln90b/operation/approaches/#replacing-an-approach)).

## Where the APT pages are used

- [Waypoints and the database](/kln90b/operation/waypoints/): choosing and creating airports.
- [Nearest](/kln90b/operation/nearest/): the list of the nearest airports and the shortcut to it.
- [Direct-to](/kln90b/operation/direct-to/): flying direct to an airport.
- [Adding a waypoint](/kln90b/operation/flight-plans/#adding-a-waypoint): adding an airport to a flight plan.
- [SID and STAR procedures](/kln90b/operation/departures-arrivals/): loading a SID or a STAR with APT 7.
- [Non-precision approaches](/kln90b/operation/approaches/): loading and flying an approach with APT 8.
