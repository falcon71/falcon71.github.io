The APT pages of the KLN 90B show everything the database holds about one airport, one kind of information per page. APT 1 to APT 6 give the airport's data, APT 7 its departures and arrivals, and APT 8 its approaches. All of them show the same airport, which you choose on any of them.

## The APT pages: one airport on every page

The airport you enter, scan to or create on one APT page is the airport on all the others, and the unit remembers it when you turn it off. One exception is the airport of the last active waypoint: if that was an airport, the unit comes on with it, shown on APT 4. Another is an airport that you only confirm or that is the active waypoint: the ACT page and a waypoint confirmation show their own airport and leave the shared one alone.

Turn the right outer knob to the APT pages and the right inner knob to the page you want. The inner knob goes through APT 1 to APT 7 and then back to APT 1; APT 8 follows APT 7 only in an aircraft whose unit is installed for approaches. A page with more than one subpage shows a `+` in its name (`APT+3`): the inner knob steps through the subpages before it goes on to the next page, and back through them the other way. When you return to the APT pages from another group, you land on the APT page you used last.

The top line is the same on every page except the APT 3 runway diagram, which has no top line. It shows the identifier of the airport and, at the right end, `nr` and a rank when you reached the airport through the <Term t="Nearest list">nearest list</Term>. A `›` in the first column marks the airport as the <Term t="Active waypoint">active waypoint</Term>; it flashes during <Term t="Waypoint alerting">waypoint alerting</Term>.

### Choosing the airport

To enter an identifier, press the right <kbd>CRSR</kbd> and turn the right knobs: the inner knob changes the character under the cursor, and the outer knob moves to the next one. You do not press <kbd>ENT</kbd>. After every character the unit shows the first airport, in alphabetical order, whose identifier begins with the characters you have entered, and fills in the remaining characters. Press the right <kbd>CRSR</kbd> again to turn the cursor off. [Controls and display](/kln90b/unit/controls-and-display/#entering-data-with-the-cursor) explains the cursor and the entry of characters.

To page through the airports instead, pull out the right inner knob and turn it; [Controls and display](/kln90b/unit/controls-and-display/) describes the scan. Your user airports are in the scan, ordered with the others by identifier. Turning back past the first airport leads into the nearest list at its last entry, and turning clockwise past the last nearest airport leads to the first airport of the database; within the nearest list each click steps one rank.

Another way in is the nearest list itself: press <kbd>MSG</kbd> and then <kbd>ENT</kbd>, and APT 1 shows the nearest airport as `nr 1`. An airport of the list keeps its `nr` on all APT pages. [Nearest](/kln90b/operation/nearest/) explains how the list is built and how to reach it.

### An identifier that no airport has

When the identifier matches no airport, every APT page offers to create one.

<KlnScreen screen="apt-create-new-wpt" />

Turn the right outer knob to `PRES POS?` or `USER POS?` and press <kbd>ENT</kbd>: the first creates the airport at the position of the aircraft, the second opens APT 1 for a position you enter. Either way the new <Term t="User waypoint">user airport</Term> appears on APT 1, whichever page made the offer. A user airport has no name, no city and no frequencies, and one runway of unknown length. [Waypoints and the database](/kln90b/operation/waypoints/) covers creating user waypoints, their limit and deleting them.

### The ACT page and the waypoint confirmation

The ACT page of an active airport shows these pages for that airport, named ACT 1 and so on. Its top line adds the number of the airport in FPL 0, the same number FPL 0 shows (blank for a direct-to waypoint that is not in FPL 0), and an `A` for airport at the right end, and the identifier cannot be changed there. The scan steps through the legs of the active flight plan instead of through the database.

When you enter an airport identifier anywhere else (on the Direct To page, in a flight plan, on REF), the right half shows its APT 1 page with `ent` in the status line, and you can step through the other pages of that airport with the right inner knob before you approve it with <kbd>ENT</kbd>. The cursor and the scan are off on these pages. [Waypoints and the database](/kln90b/operation/waypoints/) describes both cases.

Nothing on the left half changes when you choose an airport. The other way round, pressing <DirectToKey /> with an APT page on the right makes the Direct To page propose the airport that the APT page shows.

## APT 1: name, position and airspace

APT 1 shows the name of the airport, its position and the airspace it lies in. It is the page where you check that you have the right airport, and where a new user airport ends up.

<KlnScreen screen="apt-1-ksea" />

The cursor stops on the four characters of the identifier, on the rank `nr` while the page shows one, and on the position of a user airport. The right outer knob moves it from field to field and stops at the first and the last field. It does not wrap around, and each time you turn the cursor on, it starts on the first character. You cannot change the name, the airspace row or the position of an airport of the database.

<KlnScreen screen="apt-1-ksea-cursor" />

### A nearest airport

The page changes when you reach the airport through the nearest list, with <kbd>MSG</kbd> and <kbd>ENT</kbd> or by scanning back past the first airport. An airport that you enter by its identifier shows the plain page, even when it is the nearest. The rank `nr 2` appears at the right of the top line, and the three rows below the name give the longest runway (length and surface), its lighting, and the bearing and distance from the aircraft. The unit recalculates bearing, distance and the order of the list every second, and searches anew which airports belong to the list every 10 seconds. `nr` flashes while the cursor is elsewhere.

<KlnScreen screen="apt-1-nearest" />

The page keeps the airport it shows. As the aircraft moves, the rank counts up within a second when another airport comes nearer, and it disappears within 10 seconds after the airport has dropped out of the list; the page then shows the position and the airspace again. With the cursor on the rank, the page does the opposite: it keeps the rank and shows whichever airport holds it.

<KlnScreen screen="apt-1-nearest-cursor" />

Entering another identifier turns the page back into the plain airport page.

### A user airport

A user airport shows its identifier and position and nothing else: no name, no airspace row and no type. With the cursor on, the cursor stops on latitude and longitude after the identifier, and a new value changes the stored position when you press <kbd>ENT</kbd>.

<KlnScreen screen="apt-1-user-airport" />

With `USER POS?`, APT 1 opens with the cursor on the empty latitude and no airport yet. The airport exists when you have entered both coordinates.

<KlnScreen screen="apt-1-user-pos" />

[Waypoints and the database](/kln90b/operation/waypoints/) and [Nearest](/kln90b/operation/nearest/) describe the tasks that use APT 1.

## APT 2: city, elevation, time zone and approaches

APT 2 shows where the airport is, how high it lies, which time zone it is in, and the best kind of approach it has. Use it to find out the local time difference and whether you can expect an instrument approach.

<KlnScreen screen="apt-2-ksea" />

Nothing on this page can be changed for an airport of the database; the cursor stops only on the identifier and on `nr`. The page of a nearest airport has the same body as the plain page. The time zone comes from the simulator: the row stays blank where it supplies none. The radar flag `(R)` at the end of the approach row shows that approach and departure control have radar, so that you can expect vectors; it appears only for airports whose data marks radar coverage.

A user airport shows only its elevation, as an entry, because the city, the time zone and the approaches are unknown. The cursor stops on the identifier and then on the elevation.

<KlnScreen screen="apt-2-user-airport" />

[Waypoints and the database](/kln90b/operation/waypoints/) describes the tasks that use APT 2.

## APT 3: runways

APT 3 shows the runways of the airport, first as a diagram and then as a list of lengths, surfaces and lighting. Use the diagram to see how the runways lie to each other and the list for their data.

<KlnScreen screen="apt-3-diagram-kpdx" />

The diagram is the first subpage of an airport that has runways, and the status line therefore reads `APT+3`. It shows no identifier and no top line, so the page has no field: the right <kbd>CRSR</kbd> key does nothing, and you cannot change the identifier there. Scanning works, and so does the inner knob. At an airport whose runway data lists a helipad, the diagram can stay blank, while the list pages work.

{/* kln90b bug candidate (unverified in the simulator): the APT 3 diagram throws an error and stays blank for airports whose runway data has helipad entries such as H1 (KMWH); the entries may come from the FAA extract of the screens only */}

The runway list follows: the unit lists at most the five longest runways, longest first, two runways per page. On a list page the cursor stops on the identifier and on `nr`.

<KlnScreen screen="apt-3-list-ksea" />

An airport without a runway entry has no diagram, and its one page reads `RUNWAY DATA NOT AVAILABLE`. If you enter an identifier that no airport has while a list page is shown, and then correct it to an airport that has a diagram, the unit shows that diagram and turns the cursor off, so that the next click of the right inner knob selects another page instead of changing a character.

{/* kln90b bug candidate: after an unknown identifier on an APT 3 list page, entering a valid one shows the diagram and turns the cursor off */}

A user airport has one page, which holds its single runway: the length and the surface, which you enter. It appears in the nearest list only when the runway meets the minimum length of SET 3 and, where SET 3 allows hard surfaces only, has the surface `HRD`; a runway without an entered surface counts as soft.

<KlnScreen screen="apt-3-user-airport" />

[Waypoints and the database](/kln90b/operation/waypoints/) and [Nearest](/kln90b/operation/nearest/) describe the tasks that use APT 3.

## APT 4: communication frequencies

APT 4 lists the communication frequencies of the airport. The unit only shows them; you tune the radio yourself.

<KlnScreen screen="apt-4-kykm" />

Each line holds a four-character label and a frequency in MHz with two decimals; the unit rounds the half up (128.075 shows as `128.08`). A frequency up to and including 30 MHz is a high frequency and shows in kHz without a decimal point. The page lists the frequencies in the order of the database, without sorting or merging them, so a frequency that the database lists twice shows twice. These are the labels:

| Label | Frequency of |
| --- | --- |
| `ATIS` | The <Term t="ATIS" /> broadcast |
| `MCOM` | The <Term t="MCOM" /> frequency |
| `UNIC` | The <Term t="UNIC" /> frequency |
| `CTAF` | The <Term t="CTAF" /> |
| `GRND` | Ground control |
| `TWR` | Tower |
| `CLR` | Clearance delivery, also remote clearance delivery |
| `APR` | Approach control |
| `DEP` | Departure control |
| `CTR` | A center, where it provides approach and departure control |
| `AFIS` | A flight service station of the airport (<Term t="AFIS" />) |
| `AWOS` | An <Term t="AWOS" /> broadcast |
| `ASOS` | An <Term t="ASOS" /> broadcast |
| `PTAX` | Pre-taxi clearance |

A line with another type of frequency has no label. A frequency of no type is not listed.

The page shows five frequencies. An airport with more than five has more pages, and the status line reads `APT+4`; the inner knob steps through them, and the last page shows the rest with blank lines. The cursor stops on the identifier and on `nr`, because nothing on the page can be selected. If you change the airport while a later page is shown and the new airport has fewer pages, the page shows the new identifier with blank lines until you turn the right inner knob one click counterclockwise.

{/* kln90b bug candidate: APT 4 shows blank rows when the airport changes while a later frequency page is shown and the new airport has fewer pages */}

An airport without a usable frequency, and every user airport, shows `COMM FREQ DATA NOT AVAILABLE`.

<KlnScreen screen="apt-4-no-data" />

[Waypoints and the database](/kln90b/operation/waypoints/) describes the tasks that use APT 4.

## APT 5: remarks

APT 5 holds three lines of notes of your own about the airport, which APT 5 shows for that airport from then on, for example a frequency or a procedure you want to remember. The unit keeps them when it is turned off.

<KlnScreen screen="apt-5-ktiw" />

The unit keeps the remarks by identifier: if you delete a user airport, its remarks stay, and a user airport that you create again with the same identifier shows them again. It keeps the remarks of up to 100 airports. A remark for the 101st raises `RMKS FULL` and is not stored; changing the remarks of an airport already in the list is always accepted. A line takes 11 characters, each a letter, a digit, a hyphen or a blank. The cursor stops on the identifier, on `nr`, and then on the three lines in turn.

OTH 4 (`APTS W/RMKS`) lists the airports that have remarks; you delete an airport's remarks there. An airport whose lines you have blanked out still counts as having remarks, stays on OTH 4 and counts toward the 100 until you delete it there.

[Waypoints and the database](/kln90b/operation/waypoints/) describes the tasks that use APT 5.

## APT 6: services

APT 6 is meant for the fuel, the oxygen, the customs and the fee information of the airport. The simulator holds none of it, so the page shows the same texts for every airport, user airports included, and the line for customs stays blank.

<KlnScreen screen="apt-6-ksea" />

The page has no field of its own: the cursor stops on the identifier and on `nr`, and there are no variants.

## APT 7: departures and arrivals

APT 7 lists the departures (<Term t="SID" />s) and arrivals (<Term t="STAR" />s) of the airport on the APT pages and
loads the one you select into FPL 0. You use it on the ground to load the departure, and en route to load the arrival
before the descent. APT 7 is part of every installation of the unit; only APT 8 depends on how the unit is installed.

An airport with departures and arrivals has two APT 7 pages, and the status line shows `APT+7`: the first page lists
the SIDs, the second the STARs. Turning the right inner knob clockwise from APT 6 brings you to the SIDs first;
turning it counterclockwise from APT 8, or from APT 1 in a unit without APT 8, brings you to the STARs first. An
airport with only one kind of procedure has one APT 7 page with that list.

<KlnScreen screen="apt-7-stars-ksea" />

Press the right <kbd>CRSR</kbd> to turn the cursor on. It comes on at the first entry, or at the top entry shown when
you had scrolled the list before. A list has four rows: the bottom row always shows the last entry, and the rows above
it scroll as you turn the right outer knob. The cursor flashes on an entry that <kbd>ENT</kbd> selects, and on
`LOAD IN FPL` and `APPROVE?` later on. With an expired database, each press of the right <kbd>CRSR</kbd> that turns the
cursor on shows `OUTDATED DB` on the status line; you can still select and load procedures.

<KlnScreen screen="apt-7-sids-ksea" />

An airport for which the database has no procedure the unit can fly shows this text instead of the lists:

<KlnScreen screen="apt-7-none" />

On ACT 7, when the active waypoint is an airport, the same lists of that airport appear, and you select and load a
procedure the same way. The airport identifier cannot be changed there.

### Selecting a departure or an arrival

You select a departure in the order you fly it: the SID, then the runway you take off from, then the
<Term t="Transition">transition</Term> to the en route fix. An arrival goes the other way: the STAR, then the
transition from the en route fix, then the runway you land on. The unit asks for a runway or a transition only when
there is a choice to make.

To load a departure or an arrival into FPL 0, the airport must be on the APT pages (see
[Choosing the airport](/kln90b/pages/apt/#choosing-the-airport)). Then:

1. Turn the right outer knob to the APT pages and the right inner knob to the SIDs or the STARs of APT 7. From APT 1,
   the STARs are two steps counterclockwise in a unit with APT 8, one step in a unit without it.
2. Press the right <kbd>CRSR</kbd> and turn the right outer knob until the cursor is on the procedure.
3. Press <kbd>ENT</kbd>. The unit shows the first list it needs, `RUNWAY` or `TRANSITION`, with the cursor on its
   first entry, or, when there is nothing to choose, the waypoints of the procedure (step 5).

   <KlnScreen screen="apt-7-runway" />

4. Turn the right outer knob to the runway or the transition and press <kbd>ENT</kbd>. If a second list follows,
   select its entry the same way.

   <KlnScreen screen="apt-7-transition" />

5. Check the waypoints of the procedure as they go into FPL 0, which the unit now shows with the cursor on
   `LOAD IN FPL`, and press <kbd>ENT</kbd>.

   <KlnScreen screen="apt-7-load" />

6. If the unit asks a question (below), press <kbd>ENT</kbd> to approve it. The unit loads the procedure and shows
   FPL 0 on the left side, so that you can check where it went. APT 7 shows its list again with the cursor off.

If you selected the wrong entry, press <kbd>CLR</kbd>: each press goes back one step, skips the steps the unit skipped
on the way forward, and puts the cursor on the entry you selected there before. At the list of procedures,
<kbd>CLR</kbd> no longer changes APT 7. While you select, the right cursor stays on by itself. If you turn it off and
turn the right inner knob to another page, the selection is dropped, and APT 7 shows its list when you come back.

**A transition must be selected whenever the procedure has one.** When a procedure has two or more transitions, the
list has no entry for the procedure without a transition, so you cannot load only the part that all transitions
share. A procedure with one transition gets it without a question. If the transition you need repeats waypoints that
FPL 0 already has, select it anyway and delete the waypoints that are now in FPL 0 twice by hand. When an en route
waypoint of FPL 0 is also a waypoint of the procedure, the unit reminds you with the message
`REDUNDANT WPTS IN FPL EDIT ENROUTE WPTS AS NECESSARY`. A waypoint that the procedure shares with another procedure
in FPL 0, such as an arrival and an approach that both contain a fix, raises no message, so look for such copies in
FPL 0 yourself.

The unit skips a step when it has nothing to ask:

- **The transition step** is skipped when the procedure has one transition, which the unit takes, or none.
- **The runway step** is skipped when every runway of the procedure leads to the same waypoints, because then the
  runway changes nothing in FPL 0. It is also skipped when the procedure has no part of its own for each runway.
  KYKM's YKM7 departure has neither a runway nor a transition to choose, so <kbd>ENT</kbd> on it shows its waypoints at
  once.

Each entry of the runway list is <Term t="RW" /> and the runway. Parallel runways that lead to the same waypoints share
one entry with `B` for the parallels, such as `RW10B`, and selecting it loads the first of them. Runways of different
numbers always have their own entries.

### Questions before the load

When you press <kbd>ENT</kbd> on `LOAD IN FPL`, the unit loads at once if FPL 0 already contains the airport and holds
no procedure of the same kind. If the airport is not in FPL 0, also when FPL 0 is empty, it asks whether to add the
airport with the procedure:

<KlnScreen screen="apt-7-add-airport" />

If FPL 0 already holds a procedure of the same kind, a departure when you load a departure or an arrival when you load
an arrival, the unit asks whether to replace it, also when it is the same procedure. This is then the only question:
if the airport is missing, the unit adds it without asking. A procedure of the other kind does not count: loading an
arrival while FPL 0 holds a departure asks nothing about the departure.

<KlnScreen screen="apt-7-replace" />

Press <kbd>ENT</kbd> to approve the question, or <kbd>CLR</kbd> to go back to the waypoints with FPL 0 unchanged.

### Where the procedure goes in FPL 0

The unit inserts a departure right after its airport in FPL 0 and an arrival right in front of its airport, before an
approach that is already in front of the airport. An airport the unit adds goes to the start of FPL 0 for a departure
and to the end for an arrival. How a loaded procedure appears in FPL 0, how you change or delete it, and what the unit
does when FPL 0 has no room are explained in [Flight plans](/kln90b/operation/flight-plans/).

### What the unit keeps of a procedure

The unit flies procedures as a series of waypoints, so it keeps what ends at a waypoint and leaves out the rest:

- **Procedures it cannot fly are not listed:** a SID or STAR with an <Term t="RF leg" /> (a fixed-radius turn), one
  that needs <Term t="RNP AR" />, and one in which no leg ends at a waypoint. Before the flight, check that the
  departure, arrival and approach you plan to fly are in the lists; if one is missing, enter its waypoints in FPL 0 by
  hand.
- **Legs that end at no waypoint** are dropped: headings, climbs on a course to an altitude, vectors. From runway 34C,
  KSEA's BANGR9 starts at `NESOE`; you fly the climb from the runway by heading.
- **Holding patterns and procedure turns** are dropped; their waypoint stays. When that waypoint is the active waypoint
  and you are within 4 NM of it in leg mode, the unit raises the message `IF REQUIRED SELECT OBS`, so that you fly the
  hold or the turn in OBS mode.
- **Altitude and speed restrictions** are not loaded; read them from the chart.
- **DME arcs** stay. The unit replaces the waypoint before the arc with an arc entry that it computes when you select
  the procedure: the point of the arc nearest the aircraft, or the published start of the arc when the aircraft is not
  abeam the arc. The entry is named `D`, the radial from the VOR in three digits and a letter for the distance, A for
  1 NM to Z for 26 NM: `D338M` lies on the 338° radial at 13 NM. Beyond 26 NM the name is the first two letters of the
  VOR and the radial. An entry computed on the ground or far from the arc therefore lies where the aircraft was then;
  you can move it to your present track later from FPL 0, as
  [Non-precision approaches](/kln90b/operation/approaches/) explains.

Flying a departure or an arrival, and editing it in FPL 0, is covered in
[Departures and arrivals](/kln90b/operation/departures-arrivals/).

## APT 8: approaches

<Callout kind="availability">
APT 8 is available only if the unit is installed for non-precision approaches. Not every aircraft has such an
installation. Without it, the APT pages end at APT 7, and the unit has no ACT 8 page either.
</Callout>

APT 8 lists the non-precision approaches of the airport on the APT pages and loads the one you select, with its missed
approach, into FPL 0. You use it en route before the descent, usually after loading the arrival on APT 7.

<KlnScreen screen="apt-8-approaches-ksea" />

The list has five rows, and the bottom row always shows the last approach. Press the right <kbd>CRSR</kbd> to turn the
cursor on: it comes on at the first approach, or at the top approach shown on a scrolled list. As on APT 7, an expired
database shows `OUTDATED DB` on the status line each time you turn the cursor on.

Each approach has a short name of eight characters in the list and a header that heads its waypoints on APT 8 and in
FPL 0:

| Approach | In the list | Header |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <Term t="RNAV" /> (GPS) to runway 34L | `RNAV 34L` | `R34L-KSEA` |
| GPS to a runway | `GPS` and the runway | `G`, the runway and the airport |
| VOR to runway 21 | `VOR  21` | `V21-KGEG` |
| VOR/DME to runway 27 | `VORD 27` | `D27-KYKM` |
| NDB or NDB/DME to runway 32R | `NDB  32R` | `N32R-KMWH` |
| VOR-A or VOR/DME-A (no runway) | `VOR-A` | `VOR-A-KYKM` |
| NDB-B (no runway) | `NDB-B` | `NDB-B-KHIO` |

An RNAV approach without a runway shows `RNV` and its letter, such as `RNVA`. The letter of an approach to a runway is
never shown, so RNAV (GPS) Y RWY 19 and RNAV (GPS) Z RWY 19 at KRLD both read `RNAV 19` and `R19-KRLD`. Tell them
apart by their IAFs and waypoints before you load one.

The unit offers RNAV (GPS) approaches that have <Term t="LNAV" /> minima, and GPS, VOR, VOR/DME, NDB and NDB/DME
approaches. It leaves out ILS, localizer, LDA and SDF approaches, RNAV approaches that have only LPV or LNAV/VNAV
minima, approaches that need RNP AR, and approaches with an RF leg anywhere, the missed approach included. Of KSEA's
approaches, the list shows the six RNAV approaches. An airport without an approach the unit can fly shows this:

<KlnScreen screen="apt-8-none" />

On ACT 8, when the active waypoint is an airport, the same list of that airport appears, without the label
<Term t="IAP" />, and you select and load an approach the same way.

### Selecting an approach

You select an approach, then its <Term t="IAF" />, then you load it.

To load an approach into FPL 0, the airport must be on the APT pages (see
[Choosing the airport](/kln90b/pages/apt/#choosing-the-airport)). Then:

1. Turn the right outer knob to the APT pages and the right inner knob to APT 8, one step counterclockwise from APT 1.
2. Press the right <kbd>CRSR</kbd> and turn the right outer knob until the cursor is on the approach.
3. Press <kbd>ENT</kbd>. The unit shows the list after `IAF` with the cursor on the first entry, or, when the approach
   has one transition or none, its waypoints (step 5).

   <KlnScreen screen="apt-8-iaf" />

4. Turn the right outer knob to the IAF and press <kbd>ENT</kbd>.
5. Check the waypoints of the approach, which the unit now shows from that IAF to the end of the missed approach with
   the cursor on `LOAD IN FPL`: turn the right outer knob counterclockwise to scroll the list, and clockwise back down
   to `LOAD IN FPL`. The list stays scrolled.

   <KlnScreen screen="apt-8-load" />

   <KlnScreen screen="apt-8-load-scrolled" />

6. Press <kbd>ENT</kbd>.
7. If the unit asks a question (below), press <kbd>ENT</kbd> to approve it. The unit loads the approach and shows
   FPL 0 on the left side. APT 8 shows its list again with the cursor off.

<kbd>CLR</kbd> goes back one step at a time, as on APT 7, and puts the cursor on the IAF or the approach you selected
before.

**An approach starts only at an IAF.** The list after `IAF` offers the transitions of the approach, each beginning at
an IAF or at a fix before one, and you must select one of them. No entry begins at the intermediate fix or later, so
you cannot start the approach there. An approach with one transition skips the step and shows its waypoints at once.
Joining an approach in flight is covered in [Non-precision approaches](/kln90b/operation/approaches/).

**The transition rule applies to approaches too.** When the transition you select repeats waypoints that FPL 0
already has, for example the end of the arrival, delete the copies by hand. The waypoints of an approach cannot be
deleted, so delete the copies in the arrival or among the en route waypoints, and look for copies in the arrival
yourself: only en route waypoints raise `REDUNDANT WPTS IN FPL EDIT ENROUTE WPTS AS NECESSARY`.
[Flight plans](/kln90b/operation/flight-plans/) explains deleting waypoints from FPL 0.

An entry is named after the first waypoint of its transition. That is usually the IAF itself; where the transition
begins earlier, the IAF mark appears on a later waypoint of the list. At KSEA, RNAV 16C offers `PAE`, and its waypoint
list starts with `PAE` and marks `ERYKA` as the IAF. The list after `IAF` has five rows; on a longer list, `IAF` stays
on the top row and the bottom row always shows the last entry.

The waypoint list marks the fixes that have a role in the approach with a dash and a small letter after the
identifier: `-i` for the IAF, `-f` for the <Term t="FAF" />, `-m` for the <Term t="MAP" /> and `-h` for the
<Term t="MAHP" />, the waypoint where the missed approach ends in a holding pattern. A fix that is both IAF and FAF
appears twice, once with each mark. What the unit leaves out of an approach (legs without a waypoint, holding patterns
and procedure turns, altitude restrictions) and how it treats DME arcs is the same as on APT 7 (see
[What the unit keeps of a procedure](/kln90b/pages/apt/#what-the-unit-keeps-of-a-procedure)).

### Questions before the load and where the approach goes

The questions are those of APT 7. If the airport is not in FPL 0, the unit asks whether to add it, with
`AND APPR TO` (<Term t="APPR" /> for approach). If FPL 0 already holds an approach, any approach, the unit asks
whether to replace it, with `APPROACH` above `APPROVE?`; FPL 0 holds one approach at a time.

The unit inserts the approach right in front of its airport in FPL 0, after an arrival that is already there. An
airport the unit adds goes to the end of FPL 0.

With the approach in FPL 0, the unit arms it by itself as you near the airport. Arming and flying the approach are
covered in [Non-precision approaches](/kln90b/operation/approaches/), and the approach in FPL 0 in
[Flight plans](/kln90b/operation/flight-plans/).
