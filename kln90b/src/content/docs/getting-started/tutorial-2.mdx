This flight takes you from Yakima across the Cascades to Boeing Field in Seattle on an IFR route. You enter the route into FPL 0 by hand, load a <Term t="SID" />, a <Term t="STAR" /> and an <Term t="RNAV" /> approach, and clean up the waypoints they repeat. In flight you fly the SID along a VOR radial and a hold in OBS mode, read the safe altitudes near Mount Rainier and plan the descent with VNAV.

| Departure | Destination | Distance | Aircraft | Checked with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| KYKM, Yakima Air Terminal/McAllister Field, runway 27 | KBFI, Boeing Field/King County International, runway 32L | 100 NM along the flight plan | A small single-engine propeller aircraft that carries the KLN 90B | Flown with the simulator's default navigation data, AIRAC 2611; the screens show the FAA's data of AIRAC 2610 |

The route is `KYKM/27 YKM7 TITON V4 CHINS CHINS5 KBFI/R32LY`: the YAKIMA SEVEN SID from runway 27 to TITON, the airway V4 to CHINS, the CHINS FIVE STAR and the RNAV (GPS) Y approach to runway 32L. With the climb, the turn back to Yakima and the hold, the flight takes about an hour.

## What you need

You need an aircraft in which the KLN 90B is the main GPS: the KLN 90B must drive your CDI or HSI. That is the normal installation. If the needle of your CDI or HSI does not move once FPL 0 is active, choose another aircraft; [An aircraft that carries the unit](/kln90b/getting-started/installation/#an-aircraft-that-carries-the-unit) says where to find one.

<Callout kind="availability">
The approach at the end of this flight needs a unit installed for non-precision approaches, which has APT 8. Not every aircraft has such an installation. The SID and the STAR are on APT 7, which every installation has, so fly this flight all the same: you load both and fly a visual approach at the end instead of the RNAV approach.
</Callout>

The flight builds on [Tutorial flight 1](/kln90b/getting-started/tutorial-1/): it takes the power-on, the cursor and entering an identifier as known, and starts with the unit on and NAV ready at Yakima.

The unit holds no altitudes, no headings and no holding patterns of the procedures, so fly with the charts. The FAA's [chart search](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/flight_info/aeronav/digital_products/dtpp/search/) finds them by airport:

- for KYKM, the airport diagram and the SID YAKIMA SEVEN;
- for KBFI, the airport diagram, the STAR CHINS FIVE and the approach RNAV (GPS) Y RWY 32L.

For V4 between TITON and CHINS you need the FAA's [IFR en route low altitude chart](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/flight_info/aeronav/digital_products/ifr/) L-13, which shows V4 about one third from the top, at the left edge of the chart. Every altitude, and the turns and the courses of the hold at TITON, comes from these charts.

## Entering the flight plan

You enter the route into FPL 0 on the ground at Yakima, waypoint by waypoint: KYKM, TITON, CADED, CHINS and KBFI. [Creating a flight plan](/kln90b/operation/flight-plans/#creating-a-flight-plan) explains each step; [Keyboard mode](/kln90b/unit/keyboard-mode/) lets you type the identifiers instead.

**The unit cannot load an airway.** FPL 0 holds waypoints only, so you enter V4 fix by fix from the en route chart: TITON where you join it, CADED on the way and CHINS where you leave it. A route loaded from the <Term t="EFB" /> can name an airway, but the unit enters it as its fixes too ([Loading a route from the EFB](/kln90b/operation/flight-plans/#loading-a-route-from-the-efb)); this flight enters its route by hand.

FPL 0 must be empty. If it holds an old route, delete it first ([Deleting a flight plan](/kln90b/operation/flight-plans/#deleting-a-flight-plan)).

To enter the route:

1. Turn the left outer knob to the FPL pages and the left inner knob to FPL 0.
2. Press the left <kbd>CRSR</kbd>. The cursor is on the blank first waypoint position.
3. Enter KYKM with the left knobs and press <kbd>ENT</kbd>. The right side shows APT 1 of Yakima for approval. Press <kbd>ENT</kbd> again to approve it.
4. Enter TITON the same way and approve its INT page. With two waypoints in FPL 0, the unit makes the leg from KYKM to TITON active at once. Where the unit reads your indicator but does not set it, <Term t="MSG">`MSG`</Term> flashes in the status line for `ADJ NAV IND CRS TO 302°`. Leave it for now; the course changes with the next steps.
5. Enter CADED, CHINS and KBFI the same way.
6. Press the left <kbd>CRSR</kbd> to turn the cursor off.

<KlnScreen screen="tutorial-2/fpl-0-route" />

Every waypoint has a colon after its number: you entered it, so it is an en route waypoint. The active leg symbol runs from `1:KYKM` to `2:TITON` ([The active leg symbol](/kln90b/pages/fpl/#the-active-leg-symbol)). If an identifier brings up the Duplicate Waypoint page, choose the one in Washington ([Choosing between duplicate identifiers](/kln90b/operation/waypoints/#choosing-between-duplicate-identifiers)).

## Loading the SID

The SID goes into FPL 0 on the ground, from APT 7 of Yakima ([Loading a SID](/kln90b/operation/departures-arrivals/#loading-a-sid)). YAKIMA SEVEN is listed as `YKM7`.

To load YKM7:

1. Show KYKM on APT 1, as you showed Friday Harbor in Tutorial flight 1.
2. Turn the right inner knob counterclockwise to APT 7. KYKM has SIDs only, so APT 7 has one page.
3. Press the right <kbd>CRSR</kbd> and turn the right outer knob until the cursor is on `3 YKM7`.

   <KlnScreen screen="tutorial-2/apt-7-sid-procedure" />

4. Press <kbd>ENT</kbd>. The unit shows the waypoints of the SID at once, `YKM` and `TITON`, with the cursor on `LOAD IN FPL`. It asks for no runway, because every runway of YKM7 leads to the same waypoints, and for no <Term t="Transition">transition</Term>, because YKM7 has none.

   <KlnScreen screen="tutorial-2/apt-7-sid-load" />

5. Press <kbd>ENT</kbd> on `LOAD IN FPL`. The unit loads the SID and shows FPL 0 on the left side.

<KlnScreen screen="tutorial-2/fpl-0-sid" />

The SID stands right after `1:KYKM` under its header `YKM7-SID`, and its waypoints have a period after the number. `TITON` is now in FPL 0 twice: `3.TITON` of the SID and `4:TITON`, the en route waypoint you entered. The unit tells you so with `REDUNDANT WPTS IN FPL` ([`REDUNDANT WPTS IN FPL`](/kln90b/operation/departures-arrivals/#redundant-wpts-in-fpl)); you delete the copies once all procedures are loaded.

The active leg is now the leg from KYKM to YKM, the leg of FPL 0 closest to the aircraft ([Flying a SID or a STAR](/kln90b/operation/departures-arrivals/#flying-a-sid-or-a-star)). Press <kbd>MSG</kbd> to read the messages. Where the unit reads your indicator but does not set it, one is `ADJ NAV IND CRS TO 074°`: turn your CDI or HSI to 074°. Press <kbd>MSG</kbd> again to close the MSG page.

Of YKM7 the unit keeps the two waypoints, YKM and TITON. It drops the climb from the runway, which ends at an altitude, and the heading that intercepts the radial to TITON, because neither ends at a waypoint ([What the unit keeps of a procedure](/kln90b/operation/departures-arrivals/#what-the-unit-keeps-of-a-procedure)). The navigation data codes no hold in YKM7, so the unit knows nothing of the hold at TITON that the chart shows. You fly the turns toward YKM and TITON as the chart shows, and the hold yourself, as the departure and the hold below describe.

## Loading the STAR

On a real flight you load the STAR en route, once you know which STAR you get. Here you load it on the ground, so that the whole flight plan is ready before takeoff. CHINS FIVE is on APT 7 of Boeing Field as `CHINS5`.

To load CHINS5:

1. Turn the right inner knob to APT 1 and enter KBFI.
2. Turn the right inner knob two steps counterclockwise, past APT 8, to APT 7. KBFI has SIDs and STARs, and this way you come to its STARs first ([APT 7: SID and STAR procedures](/kln90b/pages/apt/#apt-7-sid-and-star-procedures)).
3. Press the right <kbd>CRSR</kbd>. The cursor comes on at `1 CHINS5`.

   <KlnScreen screen="tutorial-2/apt-7-star-procedure" />

4. Press <kbd>ENT</kbd>. The unit shows the transitions of CHINS5: `1 PDT`, `2 SUNED` and `3 YKM`.
5. Turn the right outer knob until the cursor is on `3 YKM`.

   <KlnScreen screen="tutorial-2/apt-7-star-transition" />

6. Press <kbd>ENT</kbd>. The unit shows the waypoints of the STAR from YKM, with the cursor on `LOAD IN FPL`. It asks for no runway, because every runway of CHINS5 leads to the same waypoints.

   <KlnScreen screen="tutorial-2/apt-7-star-load" />

7. Press <kbd>ENT</kbd> on `LOAD IN FPL`. The unit loads the STAR in front of KBFI and shows FPL 0 on the left side.

**A transition must be selected whenever the procedure has one.** Your route joins CHINS5 at CHINS, but every transition of CHINS5 starts before CHINS, and the list has no entry for the STAR from CHINS alone. This is the unit's correct behavior, not a fault ([A transition must be selected](/kln90b/operation/departures-arrivals/#a-transition-must-be-selected)). You select the transition that suits the route best, here YKM, and delete the waypoints that FPL 0 then holds twice.

The STAR goes after `6:CHINS`, in front of KBFI. To see it, press the left <kbd>CRSR</kbd> and turn the left outer knob down the list:

<KlnScreen screen="tutorial-2/fpl-0-star" />

`8.CHINS` repeats the en route waypoint `6:CHINS`, and `REDUNDANT WPTS IN FPL` comes again. `7.YKM` raises no message, because the YKM that FPL 0 already holds is in the SID, not among the en route waypoints, yet it would send you from CHINS back to Yakima. Read the message with <kbd>MSG</kbd>, and press the left <kbd>CRSR</kbd> to turn the cursor off.

## Loading the approach

<Callout kind="availability">
Approaches are available only if the unit is installed for non-precision approaches. Without such an installation the unit has no APT 8; skip this section and go on with [Deleting the waypoints the flight plan holds twice](#deleting-the-waypoints-the-flight-plan-holds-twice).
</Callout>

The approach goes into FPL 0 from APT 8 of Boeing Field, which lists RNAV (GPS) Y RWY 32L as `RNAV 32L` ([Loading an approach](/kln90b/operation/approaches/#loading-an-approach)). To load the approach:

1. Turn the right inner knob one step clockwise from the STARs of APT 7 to APT 8.
2. Press the right <kbd>CRSR</kbd>. The cursor comes on at `1 RNAV 32L`.

   <KlnScreen screen="tutorial-2/apt-8-approach" />

3. Press <kbd>ENT</kbd>. The unit lists the <Term t="IAF">initial approach fixes</Term> after `IAF`: `ARVAD`, `HUMPP` and `WOODI`.
4. Turn the right outer knob until the cursor is on `2 HUMPP`.

   <KlnScreen screen="tutorial-2/apt-8-iaf" />

5. Press <kbd>ENT</kbd>. The unit shows the waypoints of the approach from HUMPP to the end of the missed approach, with the cursor on `LOAD IN FPL`.

6. Press <kbd>ENT</kbd> on `LOAD IN FPL`. The unit loads the approach after the STAR, in front of KBFI.

This load raises no message, although HUMPP and AUBRN are now in FPL 0 twice: the copies stand in the STAR, and only copies among the en route waypoints raise one ([Waypoints that FPL 0 holds twice](/kln90b/operation/approaches/#waypoints-that-fpl-0-holds-twice)).

## Deleting the waypoints the flight plan holds twice

FPL 0 now holds five waypoints that the procedures repeat. Delete them from the bottom of the list up, so that the numbers of the waypoints above stay as this list gives them:

- **`11.AUBRN` and `10.HUMPP`,** the end of the STAR. The approach starts at HUMPP and runs over AUBRN, and the waypoints of an approach cannot be deleted, so the copies go from the STAR.
- **`7.YKM`,** the start of the STAR's transition. It lies behind you by the time you reach CHINS.
- **`6:CHINS`,** the en route copy of the STAR's `8.CHINS`. Delete the en route copy, so that the STAR stays complete.
- **`4:TITON`,** the en route copy of the SID's `3.TITON`. Delete the en route copy, so that the SID stays complete.

To delete each of them:

1. Press the left <kbd>CRSR</kbd> and turn the left outer knob until the cursor is on the waypoint.
2. Press <kbd>CLR</kbd>. The row asks `DEL` and the identifier with `?`.
3. Press <kbd>ENT</kbd>. The waypoint is gone, and the rows below it move up.

When all five are gone, press the left <kbd>CRSR</kbd> to turn the cursor off. [Deleting a waypoint](/kln90b/operation/flight-plans/#deleting-a-waypoint) explains the steps, and how to keep a waypoint you selected by mistake. FPL 0 now holds 15 waypoints and reads from the top:

<KlnScreen screen="tutorial-2/fpl-0-done" />

Further down, the STAR `CHINS5-STAR` holds `5.CHINS` and `6.RADDY`, and the approach follows with its header `R32L-KBFI`:

<KlnScreen screen="tutorial-2/fpl-0-done-approach" />

After `8 COVOG` come `9 AUBRN`, `10 DUANE-F`, the runway `11 RW32L-M`, the fence `*NO WPT SEQ`, the missed approach `12 FEVAS`, `13 GRRAM` and `14 LOFAL-H`, and the airport `15:KBFI` ([The approach in FPL 0](/kln90b/operation/approaches/#the-approach-in-fpl-0)). Compare the waypoints with your charts once more before you go on.

**Without an approach** in FPL 0, delete only `7.YKM`, `6:CHINS` and `4:TITON`. The STAR keeps HUMPP and AUBRN, and FPL 0 ends with `7.HUMPP`, `8.AUBRN` and `9:KBFI`.

## The flight plan in the EFB

<Callout kind="sim-only">
The real unit has no EFB. In the simulator, the EFB can ask the unit for FPL 0.
</Callout>

With FPL 0 complete, you can show it in the simulator's EFB as a reference for the flight ([Sending FPL 0 to the EFB](/kln90b/operation/flight-plans/#sending-fpl-0-to-the-efb)). Open the flight planner app of the EFB, select the Route tab, click Import and choose `REQUEST FROM AVIONICS`. The unit sends KYKM and KBFI as the airports, YKM7, CHINS5 with its transition YKM and the approach with HUMPP by name, and CADED as the only en route fix, because every other waypoint belongs to a procedure. FPL 0 stays as it is. The EFB names runway 09 for the SID and runway 14L for the STAR, because the unit asked you for no runway; that changes nothing in FPL 0, and you still depart from runway 27 and land on 32L.

## The departure

YAKIMA SEVEN has you make a climbing right turn from runway 27 direct to YKM, and then follow the radial 284 of YKM to TITON. The unit holds YKM and TITON, so you fly the turns toward them as the chart shows; then OBS mode flies the radial the way a VOR receiver does ([OBS mode](/kln90b/operation/modes-of-operation/#obs-mode)).

Before you taxi, turn the right outer knob one step counterclockwise from the APT pages to the NAV pages, and the right inner knob to NAV 1. With APT 8 of KBFI on the right, the Direct To page would offer KBFI; with NAV 1 it offers the active waypoint ([The default waypoint](/kln90b/operation/direct-to/#the-default-waypoint)). Leave FPL 0 on the left.

To fly to YKM:

1. Take off from runway 27 and start the climbing right turn toward YKM as the chart says. The unit flies the leg from KYKM to YKM, which the turn takes you away from at first.
2. Press <DirectToKey />. The Direct To page offers YKM, the active waypoint, and the right side shows the VOR page of YKM for approval.

   <KlnScreen screen="tutorial-2/direct-to-ykm" />

3. Press <kbd>ENT</kbd>. The direct-to is active: NAV 1 shows the Direct To symbol before `YKM`, with the D-bar centered on a course from where you are.

The direct-to lays a new course to YKM, the way [Recentering the D-bar](/kln90b/operation/direct-to/#recentering-the-d-bar) describes, and because YKM is in FPL 0 the unit goes on along FPL 0 after it ([Flying direct to a waypoint of the flight plan](/kln90b/operation/direct-to/#flying-direct-to-a-waypoint-of-the-flight-plan)). If `MSG` flashes, read `ADJ NAV IND CRS TO` and turn your CDI or HSI to the course it gives.

To fly the radial in OBS mode:

1. Turn the course of your CDI or HSI to 284°, the radial of the chart.
2. Turn the left outer knob one step counterclockwise to the MOD pages and the left inner knob to MOD 2. With a GPS CRS switch in the aircraft, MOD 2 shows `PRESS GPS` `CRS FOR`.
3. Set the GPS CRS switch of the aircraft to OBS; the aircraft may label it LEG/OBS. MOD 2 shows `ACTIVE MODE` and `OBS 284°`, and the mode annunciation <Term t="ENR:">`ENR:284`</Term>.

   <KlnScreen screen="tutorial-2/mod-2-obs-284" />

4. Fly over YKM and turn right onto the radial, as the chart says. The unit keeps YKM as the active waypoint, because it never sequences in OBS mode, and the D-bar shows how far you are from the radial.

**Without a GPS CRS switch,** MOD 2 shows `PRESS ENT` `TO ACTIVATE` in step 2, and in step 3 you press <kbd>ENT</kbd> instead. With the switch, <kbd>ENT</kbd> does nothing on the MOD pages ([Selecting leg or OBS mode](/kln90b/operation/modes-of-operation/#selecting-leg-or-obs-mode)). The same holds for every change of course mode on this flight.

MOD 2 shows `OBS 284°` without a colon where the unit reads your CDI or HSI and takes its course. Where it shows `OBS:` with a colon, the unit chose the course itself when it changed to OBS mode; set 284° on MOD 2 then, as [The selected course](/kln90b/operation/modes-of-operation/#the-selected-course) describes.

Before YKM the TO/FROM indicator already shows FROM, because the course points away from YKM to the northwest; the D-bar shows where the radial lies and centers once you are on it. Past YKM, on the radial, turn the left outer knob two steps clockwise to the NAV pages and the left inner knob to NAV 3. At 5 NM out it looks like this:

<KlnScreen screen="tutorial-2/nav-radial" />

NAV 3 shows `OBS 284°` and a track of 284°, and `FLY` is close to zero. NAV 1 shows the bearing back to YKM, 104°, the reciprocal of the radial. `ESA` covers only the way to YKM in OBS mode, which lies behind you, so it shows the value of your own sector ([Minimum safe altitudes](/kln90b/pages/nav/#minimum-safe-altitudes)).

Once you are established on the radial, change back to [leg mode](/kln90b/operation/modes-of-operation/#leg-mode). Leg mode flies the leg of FPL 0 to TITON and gives you its <Term t="DTK">desired track</Term> as the course for your indicator.

To change back to leg mode, set the GPS CRS switch to LEG. The mode annunciation shows <Term t="ENR-LEG">`ENR-LEG`</Term>. Without a GPS CRS switch, turn the left outer knob to the MOD pages, the left inner knob to MOD 1, and press <kbd>ENT</kbd>; then turn back to NAV 3.

YKM lies behind you, so the unit makes the leg of FPL 0 closest to you active, the leg from YKM to TITON ([Changing between the modes](/kln90b/operation/modes-of-operation/#changing-between-the-modes)). On NAV 3 it reads like this:

<KlnScreen screen="tutorial-2/nav-3-leg-titon" />

<Callout kind="info">
The desired track of the leg to TITON is 290°, not 284°, although the leg runs along the radial. The radial is measured with the published magnetic variation of YKM, 21°E, which its VOR page shows; the unit measures the desired track with the variation where you are ([Magnetic variation](/kln90b/operation/modes-of-operation/#magnetic-variation)), and the selected course at TITON with the variation at TITON, both about 6° less ([The selected course](/kln90b/operation/modes-of-operation/#the-selected-course)). Every course along the radial that the chart gives therefore reads about 6° more on the unit wherever YKM is not the active waypoint, the inbound course of the hold at TITON included; with YKM active, the selected course uses YKM's own variation and reads as charted.
</Callout>

Where the unit reads your indicator but does not set it, `MSG` flashes for `ADJ NAV IND CRS TO 290°`: turn the indicator to 290°.

## The hold at TITON

YAKIMA SEVEN has a holding pattern at TITON, in which you climb to the altitude the charts give for V4 before you go on; the STAR needs at least 10,000 ft. The chart gives the hold an inbound course of 284°, toward TITON, and an outbound course of 104°; fly the turns as the chart shows them. The unit does not fly the hold, so you fly it in OBS mode with TITON as the active waypoint, and the inbound course reads 290° on the unit, for the reason [The departure](#the-departure) gives for the desired track.

The unit gives you no reminder before TITON, because the navigation data codes no hold in YKM7: change to OBS mode yourself before you reach TITON. Where the data of a procedure has a hold, the unit reminds you of it with `IF REQUIRED SELECT OBS` ([`IF REQUIRED SELECT OBS`](/kln90b/operation/departures-arrivals/#if-required-select-obs)).

To enter the hold:

1. Leave your CDI or HSI on 290°, the desired track of the leg to TITON: it is the inbound course of the hold, 284° on the chart, as the unit counts it at TITON.
2. Before you reach TITON, set the GPS CRS switch to OBS. The unit is in OBS mode with TITON as the active waypoint and the selected course 290°. NAV 3 shows `OBS 290°`, and `FLY` is close to zero: the course line runs along the radial.

   <KlnScreen screen="tutorial-2/obs-titon" />

3. Fly the hold by the chart. TITON stays the active waypoint the whole time, and the D-bar guides you on the inbound leg.

Without a GPS CRS switch, turn the left outer knob to the MOD pages and the left inner knob to MOD 2 in step 2, press <kbd>ENT</kbd>, and turn back to NAV 3.

If NAV 3 shows `OBS:`, check that it reads about 290°, as on the departure, and if needed change it on NAV 3 or on MOD 2 ([The selected course](/kln90b/operation/modes-of-operation/#the-selected-course)).

With the inbound course as the selected course, the TO/FROM indicator shows FROM only from TITON until you are abeam TITON again in the turn to the outbound leg. The outbound leg lies on the side of TITON you come from, so on it, as on the inbound leg, the indicator shows TO.

When you have reached your altitude, leave the hold on the inbound leg, with TITON ahead of you, and change back to leg mode as on the departure: set the GPS CRS switch to LEG, or press <kbd>ENT</kbd> on MOD 1 without a switch. The unit flies to TITON along the selected course and then goes on along FPL 0 to CADED. Change to leg mode before TITON: with TITON behind you, the unit makes the leg of FPL 0 closest to you active, and that can already be a leg after CADED ([Changing between the modes](/kln90b/operation/modes-of-operation/#changing-between-the-modes)). If `MSG` flashes, turn your indicator to the course of `ADJ NAV IND CRS TO`.

## En route

From TITON the unit flies V4 over CADED to CHINS, and then the STAR on to RADDY, in leg mode. It sequences from waypoint to waypoint by itself, with [waypoint alerting](/kln90b/operation/sequencing/#waypoint-alerting) before each one, and the turns along V4 are a degree or two. The header `CHINS5-STAR` is no waypoint, so the unit goes from CADED straight on to CHINS ([The procedure in FPL 0](/kln90b/operation/departures-arrivals/#the-procedure-in-fpl-0)).

Past CHINS the leg to RADDY runs south of Mount Rainier, so look at the safe altitudes. Turn the left outer knob to the NAV pages and the left inner knob to NAV 3. At 8 NM past CHINS it looks like this:

<KlnScreen screen="tutorial-2/nav-3-cascades" />

`MSA`, the minimum safe altitude of the sector you are in, shows 16,300 ft: the one-degree sector that holds Mount Rainier begins about 3 NM past CHINS. `ESA`, the highest minimum safe altitude along the rest of FPL 0, shows the same, and in leg mode it has shown 16,300 ft since Yakima, because the route crosses this sector ([Minimum safe altitudes](/kln90b/pages/nav/#minimum-safe-altitudes)). Before the sector, `MSA` showed 8,100 ft; about 19 NM past CHINS, where you cross 47°N, it falls to 9,900 ft. `MSA` and `ESA` are off-route values: they cover the highest terrain anywhere in a sector, while the route has its own minimum altitudes on the en route chart, such as 7,400 ft on V4 between TITON and CADED.

For a map, show Super NAV 5, the map on the whole screen, in actual track up. Super NAV 5 has a map orientation of its own, so it starts in North up even if you turned NAV 5 to actual track up in Tutorial flight 1 ([Super NAV 5: the map on the whole screen](/kln90b/pages/nav/#super-nav-5-the-map-on-the-whole-screen)).

To show Super NAV 5 in actual track up:

1. Turn the right inner knob to NAV 5, and then the left inner knob to NAV 5. With NAV 5 on both sides, Super NAV 5 fills the screen.
2. Press the right <kbd>CRSR</kbd>. The pop-up menu opens at the top right, with the cursor on its first entry ([The pop-up menu](/kln90b/pages/nav/#the-pop-up-menu)).
3. Turn the right outer knob three steps clockwise. The cursor is on the map orientation, the last entry.
4. Turn the right inner knob clockwise until the entry shows `TK^`, actual track up. The map turns at once.
5. Press the right <kbd>CRSR</kbd> to close the pop-up menu.
6. Press the left <kbd>CRSR</kbd>. The cursor comes on at the range scale, 40 to start with.
7. Turn the left inner knob one step counterclockwise to 30, and press the left <kbd>CRSR</kbd> to turn the cursor off.

<KlnScreen screen="tutorial-2/super-nav-5-track-up" />

The leg to RADDY runs straight up the map ahead of the aircraft symbol, with CHINS behind you. The left side shows RADDY with its distance, the mode, the groundspeed, the time to RADDY, the desired track and the actual track ([The left side of Super NAV 5](/kln90b/pages/nav/#the-left-side-of-super-nav-5)). [The map orientation](/kln90b/pages/nav/#the-map-orientation) explains the other map orientations. To leave Super NAV 5, turn either side to another page.

Before RADDY, plan the descent for the arrival with [VNAV](/kln90b/operation/vnav/) on NAV 4. CHINS FIVE has a propeller aircraft cross RADDY at 10,000 ft, and the approach has you at or above 6,000 ft at COVOG, its waypoint after HUMPP; check both on your charts. VNAV lays a vertical path from 10,000 ft down to 6,000 ft at COVOG at 3.0°. It takes your altitude from [the altitude input](/kln90b/operation/gps-receiver/#the-altitude-input) with the unit's altimeter baro setting applied, so keep that setting the same as your altimeter's ([The baro setting](/kln90b/operation/alerts/#the-baro-setting)).

To plan the descent, at 10,000 ft and 12 NM past CHINS:

1. Turn the left inner knob counterclockwise to NAV 4. Super NAV 5 gives way to NAV 4 on the left side.
2. Press the left <kbd>CRSR</kbd>. The cursor comes on at the selected altitude.
3. Enter `06000`, 6,000 ft, with the left knobs: the outer knob moves to the next digit, the inner knob changes it.
4. Turn the left outer knob to the waypoint. It shows RADDY, the active waypoint.
5. Enter COVOG with the left knobs and press <kbd>ENT</kbd>. The right side shows the INT page of COVOG for approval; press <kbd>ENT</kbd> again to approve it.
6. Turn the left outer knob past the offset, which stays `00`, onto the angle. VNAV starts at once at the angle the field shows, about `-0.9°`, the angle from where you are: it is active, and the top line shows the advisory altitude, your present 10,000 ft. The next step replaces that angle.
7. Enter `-3.0°`: turn the left inner knob until the units show `3`, turn the left outer knob one step clockwise to the tenths, and turn the left inner knob until they show `0`. VNAV starts again at 3.0°, and NAV 4 shows `VNV ARMED`.
8. Press the left <kbd>CRSR</kbd> to turn the cursor off.

<KlnScreen screen="tutorial-2/nav-4-vnav-covog" />

[Setting up a descent or a climb](/kln90b/operation/vnav/#setting-up-a-descent-or-a-climb) explains each step, and [Which waypoint VNAV can use](/kln90b/operation/vnav/#which-waypoint-vnav-can-use) why COVOG, a waypoint of the approach after the active waypoint, will do. Without an altitude input, NAV 4 asks for `FR`, the altitude to start from, first; enter `10000`, 10,000 ft, there ([VNAV without an altitude input](/kln90b/operation/vnav/#vnav-without-an-altitude-input)). Without an approach in FPL 0 there is no COVOG to choose; plan the descent to KBFI instead, as [A visual approach without approaches on the unit](#a-visual-approach-without-approaches-on-the-unit) describes.

The descent point lies about 12 NM past RADDY, about 2 NM before HUMPP and about 28 NM ahead of you. Hold 10,000 ft until then, because the unit plans the descent point from your present altitude; as it comes closer, NAV 4 counts down to it, and there VNAV becomes active ([Flying the vertical path](/kln90b/operation/vnav/#flying-the-vertical-path)).

## The descent and the arrival

From RADDY the unit flies the STAR on to HUMPP and the approach over COVOG and AUBRN to DUANE, its <Term t="FAF" />, and VNAV guides your descent to 6,000 ft at COVOG. The approach header `R32L-KBFI` is no waypoint, like the header of the STAR, so the unit goes from RADDY straight on to HUMPP. The STAR and the approach are legs of FPL 0, and leg mode flies all their turns with [turn anticipation](/kln90b/operation/sequencing/#turn-anticipation); none needs OBS mode.

Fly the descent on Super NAV 5: its field 1 shows the state of VNAV, the countdown and then the advisory altitude, beside the map ([The left side of Super NAV 5](/kln90b/pages/nav/#the-left-side-of-super-nav-5)). NAV 4 stays the page where you enter the plan.

To show VNAV on Super NAV 5:

1. Turn the left inner knob one step clockwise from NAV 4 to NAV 5. Super NAV 5 fills the screen again, still in actual track up at the range scale 30.
2. Press the left <kbd>CRSR</kbd>. The cursor comes on at the range scale.
3. Turn the left outer knob three steps counterclockwise. The cursor is on field 1, which shows `ETE`.
4. Turn the left inner knob two steps clockwise, past <Term t="XTK">`XTK`</Term>, to `VNAV`. Field 1 shows `V ARM`.
5. Press the left <kbd>CRSR</kbd> to turn the cursor off.

At 120 kt, about 8 NM before RADDY, field 1 changes from `V ARM` to `V` and the time to the descent point. 10 NM before HUMPP it looks like this:

<KlnScreen screen="tutorial-2/super-nav-5-vnav-countdown" />

Shortly before the descent point, about 5 NM before HUMPP, `MSG` flashes over the map for `VNV ALERT`. The unit raises it while neither side shows NAV 4, and Super NAV 5 does not count, although it shows the countdown ([`VNV ALERT`](/kln90b/operation/vnav/#vnv-alert)). Press <kbd>MSG</kbd> to read it, and <kbd>MSG</kbd> again to return to Super NAV 5. The message stays, and the message prompt with it, until VNAV becomes active.

About 2 NM before HUMPP three things happen at the same moment: VNAV becomes active, the unit arms the approach, and `MSG` flashes for `PRESS ALT TO SET BARO`, which comes with the arming. The descent point and the arming are independent of each other: the descent point comes from your altitude and the angle, the arming from the distance to Boeing Field, and on this route, at 10,000 ft, the two happen to lie together. What you see:

- Field 1 shows `V` and the advisory altitude: this is your descent point ([Flying the vertical path](/kln90b/operation/vnav/#flying-the-vertical-path)). `VNV ALERT` goes.
- The mode on the left side of Super NAV 5 shows <Term t="ARM-LEG">`ARM-LEG`</Term>, and the ARM annunciator of the GPS APR switch lights. The unit arms the approach by itself as you come near Boeing Field ([Arming the approach](/kln90b/operation/approaches/#arming-the-approach)); you do not press the switch.
- The message prompt `MSG` over the map flashes for `PRESS ALT TO SET BARO`, which asks you to check the baro setting before the approach ([`PRESS ALT TO SET BARO`](/kln90b/operation/approaches/#press-alt-to-set-baro)). Where an air data system supplies the baro setting, the message does not come, and you skip the steps below.

<KlnScreen screen="tutorial-2/super-nav-5-vnav-arm" />

Start the descent first, and answer the message once you are on the path. The message changes nothing in VNAV, and VNAV changes nothing in the approach.

To check the baro setting:

1. Press <kbd>ALT</kbd>. The ALT page appears on the left with the cursor on the baro setting, and NAV 4 on the right with its cursor on the selected altitude. `MSG` stops flashing: the message is answered once the ALT page is shown.

   <KlnScreen screen="tutorial-2/alt-baro" />

2. Check that the baro setting is the one on your altimeter, and if not, set it with the left knobs ([The baro setting](/kln90b/operation/alerts/#the-baro-setting)). `IND` on NAV 4 follows it. Leave the right knobs alone: the right cursor is on the selected altitude, and a change there stops VNAV.
3. Press <kbd>ALT</kbd> again. Super NAV 5 returns.

With the arming, the CDI scale factor narrows to ±1.00 NM, so the needle of your CDI or HSI becomes more sensitive; MOD 1 shows the factor ([The CDI scale factor along the approach](/kln90b/operation/approaches/#the-cdi-scale-factor-along-the-approach)).

Descend so that your altimeter stays at the advisory altitude in field 1. 4 NM before COVOG it looks like this:

<KlnScreen screen="tutorial-2/super-nav-5-descent" />

As the unit passes COVOG, at 6,000 ft, VNAV ends: field 1 shows `V OFF`, and NAV 4 clears the plan ([When VNAV ends](/kln90b/operation/vnav/#when-vnav-ends)). From COVOG on, fly the altitudes of the approach chart.

For the approach, set Super NAV 5 to the `AUTO` range scale, which zooms in as the waypoints come closer, and turn field 1 to `XTK`, the cross-track error ([The range scale and AUTO](/kln90b/pages/nav/#the-range-scale-and-auto)). To set up Super NAV 5 for the approach:

1. Press the left <kbd>CRSR</kbd>. The cursor comes on at the range scale, 30.
2. Turn the left inner knob counterclockwise, past 1, until the field shows `AUTO`.
3. Turn the left outer knob three steps counterclockwise to field 1.
4. Turn the left inner knob one step counterclockwise, from `VNAV` to `XTK`.
5. Press the left <kbd>CRSR</kbd> to turn the cursor off. The range field shows the range scale the unit chooses.

Shortly before AUBRN the course turns right. Unless the unit sets the course of your indicator itself, `MSG` flashes for `ADJ NAV IND CRS TO 311°`: read the message with <kbd>MSG</kbd> and turn the indicator to 311°. Before DUANE the course turns by about 5° more; if `MSG` flashes again, turn the indicator to the course the message gives ([`ADJ NAV IND CRS TO nnn°`](/kln90b/operation/modes-of-operation/#adj-nav-ind-crs-to-nnn)).

## The approach

<Callout kind="info">
The KLN 90B gives lateral guidance only: along the approach it has no glidepath and no vertical guidance ([Non-precision approaches](/kln90b/operation/approaches/)). VNAV is for the descent: on this flight it ends at COVOG and gives no path for the final approach ([VNAV](/kln90b/operation/vnav/)). Fly the altitudes of the approach chart.
</Callout>

Shortly before DUANE, the FAF, the unit makes the approach active by itself ([Activating the approach](/kln90b/operation/approaches/#activating-the-approach)). The mode on Super NAV 5 shows <Term t="APR-LEG">`APR-LEG`</Term>, and the ACTV annunciator of the GPS APR switch lights. The CDI scale factor narrows to ±0.30 NM by DUANE and keeps it on the final approach ([The CDI scale factor along the approach](/kln90b/operation/approaches/#the-cdi-scale-factor-along-the-approach)).

1.8 NM before DUANE the `AUTO` range scale shows 7 NM, which holds DUANE and the threshold beyond it:

<KlnScreen screen="tutorial-2/super-nav-5-apr" />

If the mode still shows `ARM-LEG` at DUANE, the approach is not active, and the message page says why ([RAIM in an approach](/kln90b/operation/approaches/#raim-in-an-approach)); do not fly the final approach on the unit then, but fly the missed approach.

Past DUANE the active leg runs from DUANE to `RW32L`, the threshold of runway 32L, which is the <Term t="MAP" /> of the approach. Fly the final approach by the chart and land.

The unit does not sequence past the MAP: in FPL 0 the fence `*NO WPT SEQ` follows `RW32L` ([The approach in FPL 0](/kln90b/operation/approaches/#the-approach-in-fpl-0)). Past the threshold `RW32L` stays the active waypoint, your CDI or HSI shows FROM, and the distance counts up. Super NAV 5 draws no legs past the MAP. While the MAP is the active waypoint, the missed approach does not count for `AUTO`, so near the threshold the range scale comes down to 2 NM, and at 2 NM the map draws Boeing Field as its runways ([Runway diagrams](/kln90b/pages/nav/#runway-diagrams)). 1 NM past the threshold it looks like this:

<KlnScreen screen="tutorial-2/super-nav-5-past-threshold" />

To fly the missed approach instead, once you have passed the threshold:

1. Press <DirectToKey />. The Direct To page offers FEVAS, the first waypoint of the missed approach, and the right side shows the INT page of FEVAS for approval. It offers FEVAS because the window of Super NAV 5 is closed and its right side is no waypoint page ([The missed approach](/kln90b/operation/approaches/#the-missed-approach)).

   <KlnScreen screen="tutorial-2/direct-to-fevas" />

2. Press <kbd>ENT</kbd>. The unit flies direct to FEVAS and turns the right side to NAV 1, so Super NAV 5 gives way to NAV 5 on the left and NAV 1 on the right; turn the right inner knob to NAV 5 to bring the map back ([What the unit does](/kln90b/operation/direct-to/#what-the-unit-does)). The unit returns to `ARM-LEG`, the scale widens to ±1.00 NM at once, and `PRESS ALT TO SET BARO` comes again; answer it with <kbd>ALT</kbd> as before when you have time. {/* kln90b#513: describes the current behavior; the fix makes the message come only on the change from an en route mode into an arm mode */}
3. Fly the missed approach as the chart says. The unit sequences over FEVAS and GRRAM to LOFAL, the <Term t="MAHP" />, and where the course turns, a course message may ask you to turn your indicator.

Near LOFAL the unit raises `IF REQUIRED SELECT OBS` for the holding pattern of the chart ([`IF REQUIRED SELECT OBS`](/kln90b/operation/departures-arrivals/#if-required-select-obs)). In leg mode the unit flies over LOFAL and on to KBFI, so you hold in OBS mode.

To hold at LOFAL:

1. Turn your CDI or HSI to the inbound course of the hold from the chart.
2. Before you reach LOFAL, set the GPS CRS switch to OBS. The mode on the left side of Super NAV 5 shows `ARM` and the course, Approach Arm-OBS mode, and `PRESS ALT TO SET BARO` comes once more; answer it with <kbd>ALT</kbd>. {/* kln90b#513: describes the current behavior; the fix makes the message come only on the change from an en route mode into an arm mode */}
3. Fly the hold by the chart, as at TITON. LOFAL stays the active waypoint the whole time.
4. To leave the hold, set the GPS CRS switch to LEG on the inbound leg, with LOFAL ahead. The mode shows `ARM-LEG` again, and `PRESS ALT TO SET BARO` comes again; answer it with <kbd>ALT</kbd>. The unit flies to LOFAL and goes on along FPL 0 to KBFI. {/* kln90b#513: describes the current behavior; the fix makes the message come only on the change from an en route mode into an arm mode */}

Without a GPS CRS switch, turn the left outer knob to the MOD pages and the left inner knob to MOD 2 and press <kbd>ENT</kbd> in step 2, and turn the left inner knob to MOD 1 and press <kbd>ENT</kbd> in step 4.

## A visual approach without approaches on the unit

On a unit without approaches, FPL 0 ends with the STAR's HUMPP and AUBRN and then KBFI, as [Deleting the waypoints the flight plan holds twice](#deleting-the-waypoints-the-flight-plan-holds-twice) shows, and you fly a visual approach to runway 32L. The unit stays in `ENR-LEG` all the way, and the D-bar keeps its en route scale to the airport. At AUBRN it goes on to KBFI; unless the unit sets the course of your indicator itself, `MSG` flashes for `ADJ NAV IND CRS TO 313°`, and you turn the indicator to 313°. KBFI, the last waypoint of FPL 0, stays the active waypoint after you pass it ([When the unit does not sequence](/kln90b/operation/sequencing/#when-the-unit-does-not-sequence)).

CHINS FIVE gives a propeller aircraft no altitude at AUBRN, its last waypoint, so plan VNAV to KBFI itself, down to the pattern altitude of runway 32L, 700 ft MSL, which the FAA's [Boeing Field pilot page](https://www.faa.gov/flight_deck/bfi) gives. VNAV is advisory: its path is a plan for your descent, not a clearance. Give the path an offset of 3 NM, so that you reach pattern altitude 3 NM before the airport and not over the field.

To plan the descent to KBFI, at 10,000 ft and 12 NM past CHINS, follow the steps of [En route](#en-route) with these entries:

- the selected altitude `00700`, 700 ft;
- the waypoint KBFI, whose waypoint page you approve with <kbd>ENT</kbd>;
- the offset `03`: turn the left outer knob to the second digit of the offset and the left inner knob until it shows `3`;
- the angle `-3.0°`, as in the steps there.

<KlnScreen screen="tutorial-2/nav-4-vnav-kbfi" />

The descent point lies about 4 NM before HUMPP, and VNAV ends 3 NM before KBFI, where the advisory altitude reaches 700 ft.

Boeing Field lies close to Seattle-Tacoma, and its VFR arrival is intricate. Read the VFR procedures in the KBFI entry of the Chart Supplement, which the FAA's [Chart Supplement search](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/flight_info/aeronav/digital_products/dafd/search/) finds, and the FAA's [Boeing Field pilot page](https://www.faa.gov/flight_deck/bfi), and fly the arrival and the pattern as they say.
