The KLN 90B flies the DME arc of a <Term t="SID" />, a <Term t="STAR" /> or an approach as a curved leg around its VOR, but the arc starts at a DME arc waypoint that the unit computes once, when you select the procedure, from where the aircraft is at that moment. Nothing moves that waypoint later: to join the arc where your track crosses it, you move the waypoint yourself with `MOVE ?` on Super NAV 5. On the arc the D-bar follows the curve, while the distance, the time and the bearing to the active waypoint are measured straight to the end of the arc.

## Arcs in a procedure

Every leg that a SID, a STAR or an approach of the database flies as a DME arc to a fix around a VOR stays an arc in FPL 0. In the flight plan the arc is two waypoints: the DME arc waypoint where it starts, and the end fix where it ends. The leg between them is the arc; the leg to the DME arc waypoint and the leg after the end fix are straight legs.

The published start fix of the arc, the waypoint in front of it in the procedure, is not loaded. The DME arc waypoint takes its place, and in an approach it also takes its suffix, such as the `-I` of the <Term t="IAF" />. GROMO4 of Yakima with the transition PAPPS reaches GROMO, on the 250 radial of the VOR YKM, and then flies a 9 NM arc around YKM to OHKAR on its 087 radial. APT 7 shows GROMO4 without GROMO, and with the DME arc waypoint `D247I` in front of OHKAR:

<KlnScreen screen="dme-arcs/gromo4-waypoints" />

Two arcs in a row around the same VOR at the same radius are one arc, from the start of the first to the end of the last, and the fixes between them are left out. An arc that follows an arc of another radius keeps the end fix of the first arc, and the unit puts the DME arc waypoint of the second at its published start; a straight leg joins the two. Which procedures the unit offers at all, and what else it drops of them, is in [What the unit keeps of a procedure](/kln90b/operation/departures-arrivals/#what-the-unit-keeps-of-a-procedure).

In FPL 0 the arc of GROMO4 reads like this after the load, with the leg from the airport to the DME arc waypoint active:

## The DME arc waypoint

The unit computes the DME arc waypoint when the waypoints of the procedure appear on APT 7 or APT 8, the last step of the selection ([Loading a SID](/kln90b/operation/departures-arrivals/#loading-a-sid), [Loading a STAR](/kln90b/operation/departures-arrivals/#loading-a-star), [Non-precision approaches](/kln90b/operation/approaches/)). It uses the position of the aircraft at that moment, and only that one:

- **The aircraft is abeam the arc:** its radial from the VOR lies between the first and the last radial of the arc. The DME arc waypoint is then the point of the arc on the aircraft's radial, the point of the arc nearest the aircraft. How far the aircraft is from the VOR does not matter.
- **The aircraft is not abeam the arc.** The DME arc waypoint is the published start of the arc, on its first radial at its radius.

The DME arc waypoint of an arc that follows an arc of another radius is always at its published start.

<DmeArcEntry caption="Where the unit puts the DME arc waypoint of an arc around the VOR ABC, from its 250 radial to its 130 radial. Left: the aircraft is on the 200 radial, inside the shaded sector of the arc, so the waypoint is the point of the arc on that radial, D200J; the unit does not fly the dashed part. Right: the aircraft is outside the sector, so the waypoint is the published start, D250J. Either way the published start fix ARCBG is gone." />

The unit does not compute the waypoint again: not when you press <kbd>ENT</kbd> on `LOAD IN FPL`, and not while you fly. Only stepping back with <kbd>CLR</kbd> and selecting the last step again computes it anew, from where the aircraft is then.

<Callout kind="info">
A procedure selected on the ground or far from the arc keeps a DME arc waypoint that fits where the aircraft was at the selection, not the way you will fly to the arc. Yakima airport lies on the 247 radial of YKM, between the radials of the arc of GROMO4, so GROMO4 selected before takeoff starts its arc at `D247I`, whichever heading you are given after takeoff. Look at the waypoint before you reach the arc, and move it ([Moving the DME arc waypoint](#moving-the-dme-arc-waypoint)).
</Callout>

### The name of a DME arc waypoint

The name of a DME arc waypoint says where it lies on the arc:

- **Up to 26 NM:** `D`, the magnetic radial of the waypoint from the VOR in three digits, and a letter for the radius of the arc in whole NM, `A` for 1 NM to `Z` for 26 NM. `D247I` lies on the 247 radial of an arc of 9 NM.
- **Over 26 NM:** the first two letters of the VOR's identifier and the radial in three digits.

The letter is the radius of the arc, never your distance from the VOR. The radial is measured with the published magnetic variation of the VOR, so it reads like the radial on the chart.

## Moving the DME arc waypoint

You move the DME arc waypoint to join the arc where your present track crosses it: when you fly a heading to the arc, on a vector or after a departure that the unit does not fly, and the DME arc waypoint that the unit computed lies elsewhere. `MOVE ?` takes your present track, extends it ahead as a straight line, and moves the DME arc waypoint to the point where that line meets the arc between its first and last radial, the nearest such point ahead of the aircraft. The waypoint gets the name of its new radial, and the unit works out the leg to it and the turn onto the arc anew.

<DmeArcMove caption="MOVE ? with the aircraft on a track of 080° that crosses the arc from outside. The track is extended until it meets the arc between its radials, and the DME arc waypoint moves from D250J, the published start, to that point, D216J, named for its radial. The unit does not fly the dashed part of the arc." />

The move takes the track at the moment you press <kbd>ENT</kbd>, so fly the heading to the arc first and let the track settle. Without a track, on the ground or below a groundspeed of 2 kt, the unit cannot move the waypoint ([`NO INTRCEPT`](#no-intrcept)).

`MOVE ?` on Super NAV 5 works for the arc of every procedure. To join the arc where you are:

1. Fly a heading that crosses the arc between its first and last radial.
2. Select NAV 5 on the left side and on the right side. The unit shows Super NAV 5.
3. Pull the right inner knob. The window at the bottom right shows the active waypoint ([The window](/kln90b/pages/nav/#the-window)).
4. Turn the right inner knob to the DME arc waypoint.
5. Press <kbd>CLR</kbd>. The window shows `MOVE ?`. If you selected the wrong waypoint, press <kbd>CLR</kbd> again or turn the right inner knob.

   <KlnScreen screen="dme-arcs/super-nav-5-move" />

6. Press <kbd>ENT</kbd>. The DME arc waypoint moves to where your track meets the arc, and the window shows its new name.
7. Push the right inner knob in to close the window.

In the example the aircraft flies south-west after takeoff from Yakima, and its track meets the arc on the 215 radial: `D247I` becomes `D215I`.

<KlnScreen screen="dme-arcs/fpl-0-moved" />

The leg to the moved waypoint still starts at the waypoint in front of it, so the D-bar shows how far you are from that leg, not from your track. To have the D-bar lead you along your track to the arc, fly direct to the moved waypoint ([Direct-to](/kln90b/operation/direct-to/)); the unit goes on along the arc from there. A move while you are already on the arc changes the waypoint, not the arc you fly.

### `MOVE ?` in FPL 0

<Callout kind="availability">
Approaches, and with them `MOVE ?` in FPL 0, are available only if the unit is installed for non-precision approaches. Not every aircraft has such an installation.
</Callout>

FPL 0 offers `MOVE ?` on the DME arc waypoint of an approach, where <kbd>CLR</kbd> on any other waypoint of the approach answers `INVALID DEL`. To move the DME arc waypoint of an approach in FPL 0:

1. Turn the left outer knob to the FPL pages and the left inner knob to FPL 0.
2. Press the left <kbd>CRSR</kbd> and turn the left outer knob until the cursor is on the DME arc waypoint.
3. Press <kbd>CLR</kbd>. The row shows `MOVE` and a question mark. If it is the wrong row, press <kbd>CLR</kbd> again.

   <KlnScreen screen="dme-arcs/fpl-0-move-approach" />

4. Press <kbd>ENT</kbd>. The DME arc waypoint moves to where your track meets the arc, and the row shows its new name.
5. Press the left <kbd>CRSR</kbd> to turn the cursor off.

**Note:** FPL 0 offers `MOVE ?` for an approach only. On the DME arc waypoint of a SID or a STAR, <kbd>CLR</kbd> asks `DEL` and the identifier, as on any waypoint, and <kbd>ENT</kbd> deletes the waypoint, and the arc with it ([The DME arc waypoint as a waypoint](#the-dme-arc-waypoint-as-a-waypoint)). Move the waypoint of a SID or a STAR on Super NAV 5.

### `NO INTRCEPT`

`NO INTRCEPT` means that the unit found no point to move the DME arc waypoint to: your track, extended ahead, does not meet the arc between its first and last radial, or the unit has no track because the groundspeed is below 2 kt. The DME arc waypoint stays where it was. Turn to a heading that crosses the arc within its radials, let the track settle, and select `MOVE ?` again.

<KlnScreen screen="dme-arcs/no-intrcept" />

The message replaces the middle part of the status line for about 5 seconds. Super NAV 5 has no status line, so there the message prompt `MSG` flashes over the map instead, and the message page shows `NO INTRCEPT` ([Messages](/kln90b/operation/messages/)).

## Flying the arc

You fly to the DME arc waypoint on a straight leg like any other. The unit alerts you before it and anticipates the turn onto the arc, as at any waypoint with a turn, and then makes the arc the active leg ([Sequencing and turn anticipation](/kln90b/operation/sequencing/)).

On the arc the unit guides you along the curve. The D-bar and the cross-track error show how far you are from the arc, and the <Term t="DTK" /> is the course of the arc at the point abeam the aircraft, so it turns steadily as you fly around the VOR. Keep the D-bar centered as on any leg. At the end fix the unit sequences to the next leg with turn anticipation, worked out from the course of the arc at the end fix.

### Distance, time and bearing on the arc

On an arc, `DIS`, <Term t="ETE">`ETE`</Term> and <Term t="BRG">`BRG`</Term> are measured straight to the end fix, across the arc, not along it. The distance is shorter than the way you still fly, the time is too short, and the bearing differs from the desired track by up to half the angle of the arc ahead. This holds on NAV 1, Super NAV 1, Super NAV 5 and the D/T pages, and for the distance to the destination too. Fly the arc by the D-bar and the desired track, never by the bearing.

In the example the aircraft is on the 200 radial of the 9 NM arc of GROMO4, with 113° of arc and about 18 NM still to fly to OHKAR. NAV 1 shows the straight distance of 15.0 NM and a bearing of 060°, while the desired track is 117°:

<KlnScreen screen="dme-arcs/nav-1-arc" />

### NAV 2 and Super NAV 5 on an arc

While the active leg is an arc, or an arc of FPL 0 lies within 30 NM ahead, counted along the legs, NAV 2 shows the VOR of the arc in place of the nearby VOR, with your radial and distance from it. On the arc the distance is the radius of the arc, so you can fly the arc by it as by a DME. This needs an active leg of FPL 0.

<KlnScreen screen="dme-arcs/nav-2-arc" />

The VOR of the arc ahead appears even where another VOR is nearer. On the way from Ellensburg to the VOR/DME 27 approach of Yakima, 10 NM south of ELN, NAV 2 shows YKM, 17.3 NM away:

<KlnScreen screen="dme-arcs/nav-2-arc-ahead" />

On Super NAV 5 field 2 shows the desired track while the active leg is an arc, and field 3 shows your radial from the VOR of the arc after the label `ARC` in the same cases as NAV 2, whatever you chose for them ([The left side of Super NAV 5](/kln90b/pages/nav/#the-left-side-of-super-nav-5)). The map draws the arc as an arc, and the part of the arc before the DME arc waypoint dashed where you join it after its published start.

<KlnScreen screen="dme-arcs/super-nav-5-arc" />

### Leaving the arc

A direct-to replaces the arc with a straight line, also a direct-to to its end fix: the unit then flies straight across to the end fix, as the bearing shows. To get the arc back, cancel the direct-to ([Canceling a direct-to](/kln90b/operation/direct-to/#canceling-a-direct-to)); the unit makes the leg of FPL 0 closest to the aircraft the active leg, and it measures an arc along the arc, so near the arc that is the arc again.

## An arc in OBS mode

OBS mode has no arcs. In OBS mode the unit flies the selected course as a straight line through the active waypoint, the end fix of the arc, and the D-bar shows how far you are from that line ([OBS mode](/kln90b/operation/modes-of-operation/#obs-mode)). A course of the arc laid through the end fix runs far from the part of the arc you are on, so the D-bar jumps the moment you change to OBS mode, often to full deflection. In the example the selected course is 117°, the course of the arc where the aircraft is, and its line through OHKAR lies 13 NM to the left:

<KlnScreen screen="dme-arcs/nav-3-obs" />

NAV 2 and Super NAV 5 no longer show the VOR of the arc, and the map draws the course line instead of the arc. Back in leg mode with the end fix ahead, the unit flies to the end fix as a direct-to along the selected course, not along the arc ([Changing between the modes](/kln90b/operation/modes-of-operation/#changing-between-the-modes)); cancel the direct-to to get the arc back, as above. Where a holding pattern follows the arc, `IF REQUIRED SELECT OBS` asks for OBS mode at its fix after the arc ([`IF REQUIRED SELECT OBS`](/kln90b/operation/departures-arrivals/#if-required-select-obs)).

## The DME arc waypoint as a waypoint

The DME arc waypoint is a supplemental waypoint that the unit creates by itself ([Waypoints the unit creates by itself](/kln90b/operation/user-waypoints/#waypoints-the-unit-creates-by-itself)). As the active waypoint it shows on the SUP page as its ACT page, with the VOR of the arc as the reference, the radial and the radius of the arc:

<KlnScreen screen="dme-arcs/act-arc-waypoint" />

The unit adds the DME arc waypoint to the user database when it computes it. OTH 3 lists it with the type `S`, and the unit deletes it at a power-off or power-on once no flight plan uses it. Two exceptions:

- **The user database is full:** the unit does not add the waypoint and shows no message. The arc works all the same, and OTH 3 does not list the waypoint.
- **A moved waypoint** is not added to the user database at all, and OTH 3 does not list it. The waypoint it replaced stays on OTH 3 until the unit deletes it as unused.

The waypoints of an arc in an approach cannot be edited ([Non-precision approaches](/kln90b/operation/approaches/)). Those of a SID or a STAR can, like the other waypoints of the procedure ([Editing the waypoints of a procedure](/kln90b/operation/departures-arrivals/#editing-the-waypoints-of-a-procedure)), but the arc does not follow the edit:

- **Deleting the DME arc waypoint** deletes the arc. The leg from the waypoint before it to the end fix is a straight leg.
- **Deleting the end fix** leaves the arc in place: the unit flies the leg from the DME arc waypoint to the next waypoint around the VOR of the arc, at the radius of the arc, wherever that waypoint lies.
- **A waypoint inserted in front of the end fix** is reached along the arc as well.

Leave the waypoints of an arc in a SID or a STAR as they are, and move the DME arc waypoint on Super NAV 5 instead of deleting it.
